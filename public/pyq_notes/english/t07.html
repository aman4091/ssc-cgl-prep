Prepositions (1): Time and Place

Error Pro bank, 260 questions · most tested here: at / on / in for time, since vs for, by vs within, at the bus stop, in Bihar, on the beach, under the surface, into, across
How to read this rule book A preposition is a small word that shows how a noun is linked to the rest of the sentence: in, on, at, of, for, with, by, from, to… Every rule has one plain line, one fresh example, and a table built from the bank (the Q column is the question number). Highlighted = the wrong part. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. CGL+ = standard SSC point not in this bank.

In, on, at for time: big to small

Rule 1 · AT a clock time, ON a day or date, IN a part of the day Q ×11

Rule: at + exact time (at 9 p.m.); on + day or date, even with a part of the day attached (on Sunday morning, on a cold night); in + morning, evening, month, year.

✗ The shop opens on 10 a.m. in Monday. → ✓ The shop opens at 10 a.m. on Monday.

CGL+ at Christmas / at Diwali (the festival period) but on Christmas Day; in time (early enough) vs on time (exactly at the fixed time); at the end of / in the end (finally).

Rule 2 · SINCE a starting point, FOR a length of time Q ×9

Rule: with perfect tenses (has / have / had been…), since + a point of time (since 2019, since morning, since she got up); for + a duration (for two years, for the last five years). Never "from" in these sentences.

✗ I have lived here from 2018 and since six years. → ✓ I have lived here since 2018, that is, for six years.

Rule 3 · BY a deadline, WITHIN a period, TILL a point, DURING an event Q ×10

Rule: by + a point (finish by Friday); within + a length (finish within two days); till / until = the action goes on up to that point; during = inside an event or period. "Across" never shows time.

✗ Submit the form by three days. → ✓ Submit the form within three days / by Monday.

Rule 8 · INTO for movement from outside to inside Q ×4

Rule 9 · ACROSS a flat thing, THROUGH a thing with an inside Q ×5

Rule: across = from one side to the other of a flat area (a river, a road, a field); through = in at one side and out at the other of something with depth (a window, a tunnel, a forest, a room).

✗ We swam through the lake to the other bank. → ✓ We swam across the lake.

Prepositions (2): Choose by Meaning

from vs with, beside vs besides, between vs among, by bus / on foot / with a stick, "for" = purpose, of vs off, beyond, against, despite, instead of
How to use this part These rules are not about fixed partners. Say the sentence in Hindi first: "ke liye" = for, "ka / ki / ke" = of, "ke alawa" = besides, "ke bagal mein" = beside, "se (bahar nikalna)" = from, "ke dauraan" = during, "ke paar / se zyada" = beyond, "ke sahare" = against. The meaning picks the word.

Prepositions (3): Verbs and Their Fixed Partners

The single biggest group: discuss / marry / resemble / enter / request with NO preposition, accuse of, prevent from, succeed in, congratulate on, comply with, agree to, warn against
What "fixed preposition" means Some verbs always take the same small word after them: accuse of, prevent from, insist on. Meaning does not help here; you learn the pair as one unit, like a word with two parts. Other verbs take their object directly and an added preposition is the error. A transitive verb = a verb that takes an object straight after it (discuss the plan).

⚠ Answer-key notes (Part 3) Q215: "offer" can take "to" when the thing comes first: offer vouchers to guests is correct. The error is "offer to their guests" before the thing. The same holds for Q62 ("offer for").
Q93: "differ with someone" = disagree; "differ from" = be unlike. Many modern writers also say "differ from someone on a policy", so the original is defensible; follow the key in the exam.
Q103, Q231: the key's reason (verb → with, adjective → to) is a coaching shortcut, not a real rule. Real rule: compare with = examine likeness and difference (a detective checking a signature); compare to = say one thing is like another ("compare her voice to a nightingale's"). The keys are still right for these sentences.
Q171: "forbid someone to do" is the classic pattern; "forbid someone from doing" is also accepted. The key's option "from attending" is the only grammatical choice offered.
Q189: "die of fright" and "die from fright" both occur; the main error is the missing verb ("nearly dead" → "nearly died").
Q51: two fixes: "against" → "to" and "lead" → "leads" (subject "lack" is singular).
