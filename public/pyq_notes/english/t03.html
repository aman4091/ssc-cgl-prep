Articles (1): A or An, and When a Noun Needs One

Error Pro bank, 128 questions · most tested: a / an by sound, first mention (a) vs known thing (the), missing article before a singular noun, fixed phrases like "by chance", "at home"
How to read this rule book Every rule has one plain line, one fresh example, and a table built from the bank (the Q column is the question number). Highlighted = the wrong part. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. CGL+ = a standard SSC rule or example not in this bank.

Three words, two jobs

An article is a small word that stands before a noun and tells us "which one". English has only three: a, an and the.

  • A / an = "ek, koi bhi". The indefinite article: the listener does not know which one yet.
  • The = "wahi wala". The definite article: speaker and listener both know which one.
  • No article = general idea, uncountable things, names, fixed phrases.
Trick: "A/an = ek, the = wahi." If you can say "ek" in Hindi, use a/an. If you can say "wahi wala" or "jiski baat ho rahi hai", use the.

Rule 1 · A or an? The first SOUND decides, not the first letter Q ×14

Rule: use an before a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u as heard) and a before a consonant sound. Look at the word right after the article, even if it is an adjective.

✗ He is a honest officer. → ✓ He is an honest officer. ("h" is silent: "on-est")

Rule 2 · A singular countable noun never stands alone Q ×7

Rule: one countable thing (a coin, a system, a model, an association) needs a / an / the (or my, this, his…) before it. A bare singular noun is an error.

✗ She bought new car yesterday. → ✓ She bought a new car yesterday.

Rule 4 · "A / an" for any one member of a class Q ×2

Rule: when a rule or advice fits every member of a group, use a / an with a singular noun (or the plural with no article). "The" would point to one particular person.

✗ The soldier must obey orders. (which soldier?) → ✓ A soldier must obey orders. / ✓ Soldiers must obey orders.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

CGL+ "The" + singular can also mean a whole species or invention in science-style sentences: "The cow is a useful animal." "The telephone was invented by Bell." In advice sentences about people, SSC wants a / an.

Rule 5 · No "a / an" before uncountable nouns, adjectives alone, or plurals Q ×7

Rule: a / an means "one", so it fits only one countable thing. Drop it before uncountable nouns (weather, temperature as a general idea), before an adjective with no noun after it, and before plural nouns.

✗ We had a lovely weather. → ✓ We had lovely weather. · ✗ one of a best players → ✓ one of the best players

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Trick: "One of" is always followed by the + plural noun (or my / his + plural): one of the pioneers, one of my friends. Never "one of a…".

CGL+ More uncountable nouns that never take a / an: advice, information, furniture, luggage, scenery, news, poetry, machinery, bread, traffic. Use "a piece of advice", "an item of news".

Rule 8 · "Kind of / sort of / type of" + noun with no article Q ×3

Rule: after kind of, sort of, type of, variety of, the noun stands with no a / an.

✗ What kind of a phone is this? → ✓ What kind of phone is this?

Rule 9 · Two titles for one person → one article Q ×2

Rule: if two nouns describe the same person, put the article only before the first. A second article makes two different people.

✓ He is a poet and painter. (one man) · ✓ I met a poet and a painter. (two people) CGL+

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
25…his girlfriend who is a singer and a dancer.…who is a singer and dancer.
96Mr. Kapoor, who is a businessman and a philanthropist, donated……who is a businessman and philanthropist…

Same idea with "the" (see the SVA rule book): "The principal and founder was invited" = one man; "The principal and the founder were invited" = two men.

Rule 10 · Where the article sits: such a, so big a, half an hour CGL+

Rule: with such, what, quite, many and half, the article comes after the word; with so, too, as and how, it comes after the adjective.

Pattern✗ Wrong✓ Right
such / what + a + adj + nouna such good boysuch a good boy · What a lovely day!
quite / many / half + aa half hour · a quite long walkhalf an hour · quite a long walk · many a man
so / too / as / how + adj + a + nounIt is a too heavy box.It is too heavy a box. · It was so big a house.
a / the + few, little—a few friends = some; few friends = almost none; the few friends he had = all of them
Trick: "A few / a little" is positive ("kuch"); "few / little" without "a" is negative ("na ke barabar"). "Few people came" = disappointing; "A few people came" = some did come.

Articles (2): The, and When to Use No Article

Superlatives, unique things, rivers and holy books, "made specific" nouns, all the / most of the, the + comparative, and nouns that take no article

The "the" map

"The" points to one known thing. Some nouns are known because there is only one of them (the sun), some because a phrase fixes them (the gold of South Africa), and some by fixed habit (the Ganga, the flute).

Rule 11 · The + superlative Q ×7

Rule: a superlative (best, -est, most + adjective) picks one out of a group, so it takes "the". A proper name itself takes no article.

✗ Mount Everest is highest peak in the world. → ✓ Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world.

Rule 12 · "A most" = very (no comparison) Q ×2

Rule: "a most + adjective" means "a very + adjective". Use it when nothing is being compared. If you see "of all", "in the world" or "ever", it is a superlative and needs "the".

✓ It was a most pleasant evening. (= a very pleasant evening) · ✓ It was the most pleasant evening of the year.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
4This is the most important question which you have to prepare very carefully.This is a most important question… (= a very important question; no comparison)
77—It was a most amazing experience for us to travel through the mountains at night. (no error)
Trick: Q7, Q80, Q88 vs Q4, Q77. Look for a comparison group: "of all…", "ever…", "in the class" → the most. No group → a most (= very).

Rule 13 · The + ordinal (first, second, last, next) Q ×2

Rule: first, second, third, last, next, only and same point to one position, so they take "the".

✗ He was first person to arrive. → ✓ He was the first person to arrive.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

CGL+ "The only", "the same", "the very" (= exact): "He is the only son." "This is the very book I wanted." But "a second chance" (= another chance) is correct.

Superfluous Expressions: Cut the Extra Word

Error Pro bank, 60 questions (Q31–60 repeat the patterns of Q1–30) · most tested: words whose meaning is already inside (reimburse back, true fact), double negatives, double conjunctions, the wrong word ("sign" for signature)
What "superfluous" means Superfluous = more than needed. A superfluous word repeats a meaning that is already in the sentence ("return back": return already means go back). Yahan galti kuch "kam" hone ki nahi, kuch "zyada" hone ki hai: find the extra word and cut it. Highlighted = the extra or wrong part. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. Question Tags (1): Build the Tag in Four Steps

Error Pro bank, 90 questions (60 tag questions + 30 repeated superfluous items) · most tested: positive ↔ negative, "isn't it" misuse, "aren't I", first helping verb, have as a main verb
What a question tag is A question tag is the short question at the end of a statement: "You are tired, aren't you?" It asks the listener to agree ("hai na?"). It has only two words: a helping verb (is, are, was, do, does, did, has, will, can…) and a pronoun (he, she, it, they…). Highlighted = the wrong tag. Q ×6 = bank questions on the rule. The mirror rule

The four-step tag builder

Trick: "Tag = helping verb + pronoun, ulta." Copy the first helping verb, flip it, add the pronoun. Bas do shabd.

Rule 1 · Positive statement, negative tag Q ×6

Rule: if the statement has no "not" (and no hidden negative), the tag is negative: helping verb + n't + pronoun. The verb in the statement must match the tag's tense.

✗ Your brother plays chess, does he? → ✓ Your brother plays chess, doesn't he?

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
1Your eldest sister lives in a big city, does she?…, doesn't she?
31Your cousin practises advanced photography regularly…, does he?…, doesn't he?
8—You will participate in the marathon, won't you? (no improvement)
38—You will attend the official briefing tomorrow…, won't you? (no improvement)
19He ___ hard for the exam, didn't he?He studied hard… (tag "didn't" → positive past simple)
49He ___ several practice tests over the weekend…, didn't he?He took several practice tests…
Trick: Q19 and Q49 work backwards: read the tag first. "didn't he" = the statement is positive and past simple, so the verb is the plain past form (studied, took).

Rule 2 · Negative statement, positive tag (same helping verb) Q ×4

Rule: if the statement has "not / n't", the tag is positive, and it keeps the same helping verb: can't → can, isn't → is. Do not swap the helping verb for another one.

✗ You mustn't be late, will you? → ✓ You mustn't be late, must you?

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
11We can't live without water, will we?…, can we?

7She is not ready for marriage, isn't it?…, is she? (negative → positive; she → she)
37She is not comfortable speaking in front of a large audience…, isn't it?…, is she?

Rule 3 · "Isn't it?" is not a universal tag Q ×6

Rule: in Indian speech "isn't it?" goes after every sentence. In exam English the tag must copy the sentence's own helping verb and its subject. "Isn't it" fits only "It is…".

✗ They have finished, isn't it? → ✓ They have finished, haven't they?

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

9The tree was uprooted by the storm last evening, isn't it?…, wasn't it? (was → wasn't)
39The train was delayed for several hours…, isn't it?…, wasn't it?

Rule 4 · I am …, aren't I? Q ×2

Rule: the negative tag of "I am" is aren't I ("amn't I" is not used). The negative statement "I am not" takes "am I".

✓ I am late, aren't I? · ✓ I am not late, am I?

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Rule 5 · Two or three helping verbs → only the first goes into the tag Q ×4

Rule: in "should have married", "has been teaching", "will have gone", only the first helping verb (should, has, will) appears in the tag.

✗ They will have left, haven't they? → ✓ They will have left, won't they?

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Rule 6 · Have / has as the main verb (= own) → do / does / did Q ×3

Rule: when have / has is the only verb and means "possess", the tag uses do / does (did for "had"). When have / has helps another verb (have locked), the tag uses haven't / hasn't.

✓ She has a car, doesn't she? · ✓ She has bought a car, hasn't she?

Rule 7 · Two clauses → the main clause decides Q ×3

Rule: ignore the dependent clause (when…, because…, as…, that…). Build the tag from the main clause's subject and helping verb.

✓ He will call you when he reaches, won't he? (not "doesn't he")

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Rule 8 · "I think / I believe / I imagine + clause" → tag from the second clause Q ×2

Rule: when the main clause is just "I think / I believe / I suppose / I imagine / I know", the real statement is the clause after it, so the tag follows that clause. This works only with "I" as the subject (see Q18 above: "Nobody believes" is a real main clause).

Check yourself

"Somebody has taken my pen, ___?"

haven't they. Somebody → they; has → haven't with "they" (Rule 10).

"Few students passed the test, ___?"

did they. "Few" is negative, so the tag is positive (Rule 11, CGL+).

"Let us leave now (please allow us), ___?"

will you. "Let us" asking permission takes "will you"; "Let's" (suggestion) takes "shall we" (Rules 15, 17).

"The police have arrested the thief, ___?"

haven't they. "Police" is always plural (Rule 14).

"You'd better see a doctor, ___?"

hadn't you. 'd better = had better (Rule 17).
