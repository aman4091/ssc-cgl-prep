Verbs (1): The Right Form, the Right Word

Error Pro bank "Verb + Modals", 213 questions · most tested: wrong V2/V3 forms, lie/lay, affect/effect, hear/listen, stative verbs
How to read this rule book Every rule has one plain line, one fresh example, and a table built from the bank (the Q column is the question number in "Verb + Modals"). Highlighted = the wrong part. Bold = the fix. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. CGL+ = a standard SSC rule or example that is not in this bank.
Three words you need first V1, V2, V3 = the three forms of a verb: go – went – gone. V1 is the base form, V2 is the simple past, V3 is the past participle.
Helping verb = is, am, are, was, were, has, have, had, do, does, did, and the modals (can, will, must…). It stands before the main verb.
Object = the thing the action is done to: She laid the book on the table.

Rule 1 · V2 stands alone; V3 needs a helper Q ×5

Rule: use V2 for a simple past action. Use V3 only after has / have / had or is / was / were / been. A V3 without a helper is an error.

✗ She written a letter yesterday. → ✓ She wrote a letter yesterday. · ✓ She has written a letter.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
14When the time was over they torn off the papers.…they tore off the papers. (tear – tore – torn)
154Before the concert began, the singer sung a couple of old folk songs.…the singer sang… (sing – sang – sung)
64The district woken up to a thick blanket of snow this morning.The district woke up… (wake – woke – woken)
67As the tree came crashing down, the monkeys woke up from its sleep.…the monkeys woke up from their sleep. (past V2; monkeys → their)
104The child felt down from a great height.The child fell down… (fall – fell – fallen; "felt" is from feel)
Trick: "V3 akela nahi chalta." If you see gone, sung, written, woken with no has / had / was before it, change it to V2.

Rule 2 · Do not invent "-ed" forms Q ×11

Rule: irregular verbs have their own V2 and V3; adding -ed to them is always wrong. Some verbs keep the same form in all three (put, cut, broadcast, telecast).

✗ The bell ringed at noon. → ✓ The bell rang at noon. CGL+

Rule: some verbs share a form with another verb. Decide by meaning, and check whether the verb has an object.

Rule 5 · Hear, listen to, feel Q ×5

Rule: listen to = hear on purpose (news, music, a speech); hear = a sound reaches you without trying; feel = through the body (heat, a quake). "Listen" always takes "to" before its object.

✗ I listened a strange noise at night. → ✓ I heard a strange noise at night.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
9Every night I am in the habit of hearing the news on the radio.…in the habit of listening to the news.
72Hears to the news tonight at 9 p.m.Listen to the news tonight… (command → V1)
51Most of my friends heard the earthquake but I was unaware of it.Most of my friends felt the earthquake…
165…they clearly felt a distinct ringing sound coming from the magnetron.…they clearly heard a ringing sound…
175…I suddenly listened to a loud, unexpected crash.…I suddenly heard a loud crash. (sudden = not on purpose)
Trick: "Suddenly" + sound → heard. "Every night / the news / music" → listen to. Same pair: see (happens) vs look at / watch (on purpose).

Rule 6 · Stative verbs do not take "-ing" Q ×5

Rule: a stative verb shows a state (owning, knowing, believing), not an action, so it is not used in continuous tenses. Use the simple form instead.

✗ I am knowing him for years. → ✓ I have known him for years.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
5The scooter I am using these days is belonging to my brother.…belongs to my brother.
57My friend is owing an expensive cell phone.My friend owns an expensive cell phone.
194The businessman is owning many companies, and he is believing that…The businessman owns … and he believes that…
63—A clinical thermometer consists of a long, narrow glass tube. (no error; not "consisting")
168Had he been knowing about the changes, he would not have proceeded.Had he known… (perfect continuous → perfect)

CGL+ Stative list: know, understand, believe, think (= believe), remember, forget, own, possess, belong, contain, consist, comprise, seem, appear, resemble, love, hate, like, prefer, want, need, wish. Some have an action meaning that can take -ing: "I am thinking about it" (= considering), "She is having lunch" (= eating), "He is seeing the doctor" (= meeting).

Rule 7 · After get / become, use an adjective or V3; special V3 adjectives Q ×6

✗ He got injure in the match. → ✓ He got injured in the match.

Check yourself

"She ___ on the sofa and fell asleep." laid or lay?

lay. No object, so the verb is "lie", and its V2 is "lay" (Rule 3).

"The murderer was ___ at dawn." hung or hanged?

hanged. For a death sentence use hang – hanged – hanged (Rule 3).

"The match was ___ live on TV." broadcasted or broadcast?

broadcast. Its V1, V2 and V3 are the same (Rule 2).

"I ___ this car since 2019." am owning or have owned?

"The new rule will not ___ students." affect or effect?

affect. After "will" you need a verb; "effect" is mostly a noun (Rule 4).

Rule: an -ing form alone is not a verb. Give each clause a finite verb (one that shows tense), and keep the tense that the rest of the sentence shows.

Rule 14 · A reported question uses statement order Q ×1

Rule: inside "asked / wanted to know / wondered", the question becomes a statement: subject first, then the verb, and no question mark.

✗ She asked me where was I going. → ✓ She asked me where I was going.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Rule 15 · "Regard as", but "make / elect / consider" without "as" Q ×4

Rule: regard, describe, define, treat, look upon, know (be known) take as before the second noun. Make, elect, appoint, choose, name, call, consider take no "as".

✓ We regard him as a hero. · ✓ They elected him president. · ✗ They elected him as president.

Rule 16 · Some verbs take the object directly; others need a fixed preposition Q ×7

Rule: describe, discuss, obey, tell, ask, reach, enter, resemble take their object with no preposition. Depend on, explain something to someone, say to someone.

✗ Let us discuss about the plan. → ✓ Let us discuss the plan. CGL+

Rule 17 · Suggest / insist / recommend that + subject + (should) V1 CGL+

Rule: after verbs of advice and demand (suggest, recommend, insist, demand, propose, order) the that-clause takes V1 or should + V1, never V + s.

✗ The doctor suggested that he takes rest. → ✓ The doctor suggested that he (should) take rest. · Same after "It is necessary / essential that he be present."

Modals: Can, Could, Should, Would, Need, Used to

Modal + V1, choosing a modal by meaning, modal + have + V3, used to vs be used to, need and dare
What is a modal? A modal is a helping verb that adds a meaning such as ability, possibility, duty or habit: can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to. It never changes with the subject (no "cans"), and the verb after it is always V1.

Rule 18 · Modal + V1 (never V2, V3, -ing, a noun or an adjective) Q ×5

Rule: the word right after a modal must be the base form of a verb. A noun (success) or adjective (aware) in that slot is wrong.

✗ He can speaks French. → ✓ He can speak French.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
26"Would you liked to have some fries, sir?""Would you like…"

Rule: an adjective (happy, ready, able, sure) cannot follow a modal directly; put "be" in between.

✗ Next year she can be able to drive. → ✓ Next year she will be able to drive.

Advance Verb (3): Participles (-ing, V3, having + V3)

Doer → -ing, receiver → V3; "while doing"; having + V3 for the earlier action; dangling participles; "It being…"
What is a participle? A participle is a verb form used like an adjective. Present participle = V1 + ing (a crying baby). Past participle = V3 (a broken toy). Perfect participle = having + V3 (Having finished work, he left). A participle phrase must describe the right noun.

Rule 20 · The noun does it → -ing; it is done to the noun → V3 Q ×10

Rule: ask whether the noun is the doer or the receiver of the action. Doer → present participle (-ing). Receiver → past participle (V3).

✗ The breaking glass was swept away. → ✓ The broken glass was swept away.
