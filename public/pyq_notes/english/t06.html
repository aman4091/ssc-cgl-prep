Subject–Verb Agreement (1): Find the Real Subject

Error Pro bank, 150 questions · most tested: words between subject and verb, "as well as / with", each / every, either–or, one of
How to read this rule book Every rule has one plain line, one fresh example, and a "more examples" table built from the bank (the Q column is the question number). Highlighted = the wrong part. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. CGL+ = standard SSC rule or example not in this bank.

The 6-step check

Trick: "Noun pe -s, verb pe nahi." A plural noun usually ends in -s, but a singular verb ends in -s: boys play, a boy plays.

Rule 1 · Singular subject, singular verb Q ×9

Rule: one person or thing (he, she, it, a name, one noun) takes is / has / was / does / verb + s; a plural subject takes are / have / were / do / plain verb.

✗ My brother work in Pune. → ✓ My brother works in Pune.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
10Children listens to the people around them for months before they speak.Children listen… ("children" is already plural)
97Children absorbs values from the adults around them.Children absorb…
26They are good friends and it don't matter who is richer.…it doesn't matter…
27It have been only through writing that people spread ideas.It has been…
35A recent government report highlight the teacher shortage.…report highlights (or has highlighted)…
36The driver kept speeding and have been caught by the police.…and was caught… (singular + past)
6…and her indifference were noticed by all.…her indifference was noticed.
65—The likely side effects of this medicine include itching. (no error: "side effects" is plural)
67The plants respond quickly to these stimulus.…to these stimuli. (these + plural noun)

CGL+ Latin/Greek plurals: criterion → criteria, phenomenon → phenomena, medium → media, crisis → crises, alumnus → alumni, stimulus → stimuli.

Rule 2 · Ignore the "of / between / for" phrase Q ×17

Rule: a phrase starting with of, between, for, in, on, over, to after the subject is extra; the verb follows the head noun before it.

✗ The colour of the walls are faded. → ✓ The colour of the walls is faded.

Words in between do not count

Rule 3 · A clause or a name in commas does not change the subject Q ×6

Rule: a "who / which / that…" clause or an appositive (", the famous actor,") after the subject is extra; the verb still follows the subject.

✗ The bridge that the engineers built last year have cracks. → ✓ The bridge … has cracks.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
19The proposal which all members passed last month have not been implemented.The proposal … has not been…
58Our class teacher, who helps all her students, are respected by everyone.Our class teacher … is respected…

138She, the victim of the kidnapping, were rescued by the police.She … was rescued…
72I, Anjali Menon, is happy to let you know…I, Anjali Menon, am happy… (verb follows "I", not the name)

Rule 4 · "As well as / along with / with" → first subject Q ×8

Rule: with, along with, together with, as well as, in addition to, besides, like, except, accompanied by, rather than do not join subjects; the verb follows the FIRST one.

✗ The minister, with his guards, were late. → ✓ The minister, with his guards, was late.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
8The boy, as well as his friends, were absent.The boy … was absent.
131The captain, as well as his teammates, are responsible for discipline.…is responsible…
62Honesty as well as discipline are required to succeed.…is required…
93The performance of the athletes, as well as the coaches, were praised.The performance … was praised.
55The Prime Minister, along with other ministers, have left for America.…has left…
69The student with his parents were asked for waiting outside.…was asked to wait…
136The boy with his two friends were caught playing games.…was caught…
95The committee members, in addition to the chairman, was invited.…were invited. (first subject is plural)

CGL+ Same rule: "The teacher, accompanied by her students, is going on a trip." "He, rather than his brothers, is to blame." "Nobody except the twins was absent."

Rule 5 · A and B → plural Q ×8

Rule: two subjects joined by "and" (or "both … and") take a plural verb. "X and I" also takes a plural verb.

✗ Ravi and his sister lives nearby. → ✓ Ravi and his sister live nearby.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

91Both the director and his senior advisers has emphasized transparency.…have emphasized…
47A tigress and her three cubs was poison.…were poisoned. (plural + passive)

121The journalist and the photographer was arrested.…were arrested. ("the" twice = two people)
132My father and I was discussing money.My father and I were discussing…
63—Shubham and I were watching a match. (no error)

CGL+ "Both … and", never "both … as well as": ✗ Both Ram as well as Shyam → ✓ Both Ram and Shyam.

Rule 6 · A and B = one person or one unit → singular Q ×5

Rule: if one "the" covers both nouns (one person with two roles), or the pair is one fixed idea (bread and butter, law and order), the verb is singular.

✗ The poet and painter are here. (one person) → ✓ The poet and painter is here. · But: The poet and the painter are here. (two people)

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
59The famous author and actor are being honoured today.…is being honoured. (one "the" = one man)
128The principal and founder of St. Joseph's School were invited.…was invited.
5The horse and carriage have been standing at the door.…has been… (one vehicle)
92Bread and butter have been your favourite breakfast.…has been… (one dish)
129Law and order are maintained strictly here.…is maintained… (one idea)

CGL+ Fixed pairs SSC repeats: slow and steady wins the race, time and tide waits for none, rice and curry is, the bread and butter of a job.

Rule 7 · Each / every before the noun → singular, even with "and" Q ×8

Rule: each or every before one or more nouns makes the verb singular, even if the nouns are joined by "and".

✗ Every cup and plate were broken. → ✓ Every cup and plate was broken.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Rule 8 · Each of / either of / neither of → singular; "every of" is wrong Q ×4

Rule: each of, either of, neither of + plural noun take a singular verb. "Every" cannot take "of": use "each of" or "every one of".

✗ Neither of my phones work. → ✓ Neither of my phones works.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
66Neither of the political parties have had much interest.…has had…
135Either of the candidates have shown leadership.…has shown…
80Every of these students speaks English fluently.Each of these students…
146Every of these paintings is displayed well.Each of these paintings is…

CGL+ Either / neither = one of two. For three or more use any of / none of: ✗ either of the five boys → ✓ any of the five boys.

Rule 9 · One of + plural noun + singular verb Q ×5

Rule: after "one of" the noun is plural, but the verb follows "one", so it is singular.

✗ One of my uncle live abroad. → ✓ One of my uncles lives abroad.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

29One of the biggest enterprise in India is declaring a lockout.…the biggest enterprises…

101One of the books that were kept on the top shelf have disappeared.…has disappeared. ("that were kept" is right: it refers to books)
149One of the girls have been selected.…has been selected.

Rule 10 · Either–or, neither–nor, not only–but also → nearest subject Q ×8

Rule: with or, nor, either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.

✗ Either the players or the coach are to blame. → ✓ Either the players or the coach is to blame.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
2Neither the doctors nor the nurse were present.…the nurse was…
61Neither John nor Simon are coming.…is coming…
130Neither the officer nor the suspect were telling the truth.…was telling…
89Neither the manager nor the employees was willing to accept it.…were willing…
39Either Ramesh or Reema have the keys.…Reema has…
143Not only the family but also the police officers was searching for clues.…were searching…
68—Either the bears or the lion has escaped. (no error)
76—Not only the boss but also all the employees were involved. (no error)

CGL+ The person also follows the nearest subject: "Either you or he is wrong." "Neither he nor I am going." Pairs are fixed: neither … nor (never "neither … or").

Rule 11 · Everyone, someone, nothing… → singular Q ×6

Rule: everyone, everybody, everything, someone, anything, no one, nobody, nothing take a singular verb; "nothing but + noun" is singular too.

✗ Everybody in the hostel were asleep. → ✓ Everybody … was asleep.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
17No one were present when I entered.No one was…

Rule 12 · None of → singular (formal) Q ×2

Rule: in exam English, "none of + plural noun" takes a singular verb; "none of + uncountable noun" is always singular.

✗ None of the milk were spoiled. → ✓ None of the milk was spoiled.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
78None of the guests were willing to stay.…was willing to stay.
144None of the rescuers were willing to climb down.…was willing…

Rule 13 · Many a + singular noun + singular verb Q ×4

Rule: "many a" means many, but grammar treats it as one: singular noun, singular verb, singular pronoun (his, not their).

✗ Many a student have failed. → ✓ Many a student has failed. · Same idea: Many students have failed.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
11Many a deep thinker believe that civilisation is not progress.…believes…
98Many a visionary leader believe that democracy lies in the people.…believes…
64Many an old story were retold.Many an old story was retold.
133Many a brave soldier have sacrificed their life.…has sacrificed his life.

Rule 14 · More than one + singular; more … than one + plural Q ×2

Rule: "more than one + singular noun" takes a singular verb; "more + plural noun + than one" takes a plural verb.

✓ More than one window was broken. · ✓ More windows than one were broken.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
94More than one applicant have claimed the terms were misleading.…has claimed…
7More persons than one has been involved in this quarrel.…have been…
Trick: Read the noun that sits right after the opening words: "more than one applicant" (singular), "more persons" (plural). The verb copies that noun.

Check yourself

"The quality of these mangoes ___ poor." is or are?

is. The subject is "quality"; "of these mangoes" is crossed out (Rule 2).

"The chairman, along with his members, ___ arrived." has or have?

has. "Along with" never joins subjects (Rule 4).

"Neither the teacher nor the students ___ ready." was or were?

were. The nearest subject, "students", is plural (Rule 10).

"The secretary and treasurer ___ absent." (one person)

is. One "the" means one person (Rule 6).

"Many a man ___ lost his job." has or have?

has. Many a + singular noun + singular verb (Rule 13).

Subject–Verb Agreement (2): Special Subjects

Quantity words, collective nouns, titles, -ing and clause subjects, who / which / that, "there" and inversion

Rule 15 · A number of → plural; the number of → singular Q ×5

Rule: "a number of + plural noun" means "many", so the verb is plural; "the number of + plural noun" is a count, so the verb is singular.

✓ A number of trains are late. · ✓ The number of trains is small.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
12The number of students attending class are falling.The number … is falling.
81When the monsoon starts, the number of tourists start decreasing.…starts decreasing.
99The number of tourists visiting monuments have declined.…has declined.
83A number of innocent people dies in wars every year.…die…
148A number of vehicles was parked outside the stadium.…were parked…
Trick: "A number = anek (many → plural). The number = the sankhya (one figure → singular)."

Rule 16 · A great / good many + plural noun + plural verb Q ×2

Rule: "a great many" and "a good many" mean "very many": plural noun, plural verb, and the "a" must stay.

✗ Good many shops was shut. → ✓ A good many shops were shut.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
22Great many students who are good at other subjects do badly in English.A great many students…
108A great many people in big cities faces stress.…face…

Rule 17 · Most / some / half / plenty / a lot of → look at the noun after "of" Q ×6

Rule: with most, some, all, half, part, plenty, a lot, fractions, percentages and "millions of", the verb follows the noun after "of": uncountable or singular → singular, plural → plural.

✓ Half of the cake is eaten. · ✓ Half of the mangoes are rotten.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
46Most of the work of this NGO are of little help.…is… (work = uncountable)
120Plenty of the news on social media are fake.…is…
118Three-fourths of the milk we bought have not been used.…has not been used.
42A large amount of money he invested were wasted.…was wasted.
40…what millions of youngsters aspires to be.…aspire…
33Most of the addict, a whopping 85%, are below 25.Most of the addicts… (countable → plural noun)

CGL+ "A lot of people are…", "A lot of sugar is…", "The majority of voters are…", "Forty per cent of the land is dry."

Rule 18 · Uncountable nouns are always singular Q ×2

Rule: news, information, advice, furniture, luggage, equipment, scenery, money, work have no plural form and take a singular verb.

✗ The information you gave were wrong. → ✓ The information … was wrong.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
37The news of his involvement in crime have shocked everybody.…has shocked…
115The news of the incident reported from Kolkata have shaken the nation.…has shaken…

CGL+ ✗ informations, furnitures, sceneries, advices, luggages. To count them: a piece of advice, two items of furniture.

Rule 19 · Time, money, distance, weight as one amount → singular Q ×2

Rule: a plural number used as one total amount (a sum, a period, a distance, a weight) takes a singular verb.

✓ Ten thousand rupees is a big sum for me. · ✓ Twenty kilograms is the baggage limit.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
3Five hours are too short a time to judge someone.Five hours is…
90I don't think two miles are a long distance at your age.…two miles is a long distance… (note "a")

CGL+ Separate items stay plural: "Five rupee coins were lying on the floor."

Rule 20 · Collective nouns: one unit or many members? Q ×5

Rule: team, staff, jury, committee, cast, crowd, family, class, neighbourhood take a singular verb when they act as one unit, and a plural verb when the members act separately or disagree.

102The jury has given their verdict, though it was not unanimous.The jury have given their verdict. (split members)

Rule 21 · Names and titles are singular Q ×4

Rule 23 · The + adjective = a group of people → plural Q ×2

Rule: the rich, the poor, the old, the oppressed, the brave mean "people who are …", so the verb is plural.

✗ The rich is getting richer. → ✓ The rich are getting richer.

Rule 24 · An -ing word or "to + verb" as subject → singular Q ×6

✗ Reading novels relax me. → ✓ Reading novels relaxes me. · ✓ To err is human. CGL+

Rule 25 · A whole clause as subject (What… / That… / Whether…) → singular Q ×4

Rule: a clause starting with what, that, whether, why or how can be the subject; it counts as one thing, so the verb is singular. "It" standing in for such a clause is also singular.

Rule 27 · "There is / there are": the subject comes after the verb Q ×4

Rule 28 · Inversion: place phrase first, subject after the verb Q ×2

Rule: when a sentence opens with a place phrase (Among…, Near…, Under…, Here…), the verb comes before the subject; find the subject after it.

✓ Here comes the bus. · ✓ Here come the buses. CGL+

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
85Among those broken chairs were lying a brand-new table.…was lying a brand-new table.
150Near the riverbank were lying a wooden boat.…was lying a wooden boat.

Both options also offer "laying": lie – lay – lain – lying = to rest; lay – laid – laid – laying = to put something down. A boat "is lying", it is not "laying".

Rule 31 · Kind of / sort of / type of CGL+

Rule: this kind of + singular noun + singular verb; these kinds of + plural noun + plural verb.

Check yourself

"A number of students ___ absent; the number of absentees ___ ten." are / is?

are, is. A number of = many (plural); the number of = a count (singular).

"Economics ___ my favourite subject." is or are?

is. Subject names in -ics are singular (Rule 22).

"The committee ___ divided in their opinions." was or were?

were. "Divided" and "their" show the members acting separately (Rule 20).

"He is one of the boys who ___ won a medal." has or have?

have. "Who" refers to "boys" (Rule 26).

"There ___ been many changes." has or have?

have. The real subject, "many changes", comes after the verb (Rule 27).
