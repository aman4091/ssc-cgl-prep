Adjectives (1): Right Form, Right Place, Right Quantity

Error Pro bank, 145 questions · most tested: look-alike adjectives, few / a few / little, adjective after a linking verb, some / any / much / many, word order
How to read this rule book Every rule has one plain line, one fresh example, and a table built from the bank (the Q column is the question number). Highlighted = the wrong part. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. CGL+ = standard SSC rule or example not in this bank.
First, two words An adjective describes a noun: a tall boy, the boy is tall. An adverb describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb: he runs fast, very tall. Most adjective errors in SSC are simply "adjective ki jagah adverb laga diya" or the other way round.

Adjective or adverb? The 3-question test

Rule 1 · After a linking verb, use an adjective, not an adverb Q ×6

Rule: be, become, seem, look, appear, feel, taste, smell, sound, grow, remain are often linking verbs (they join the subject to a description, no action). The word after them describes the subject, so it is an adjective.

✗ The milk smells badly. → ✓ The milk smells bad.

104Driving on the right is counterintuitively to a man used to Indian roads.…is counterintuitive to…

75Priya had strong enough to fight all odds.Priya was strong enough… (subject + linking verb + adjective)

CGL+ Same verb, two jobs: "She looks sad" (linking → adjective) · "She looked sadly at the ruins" (action → adverb). "He feels bad" ✓, not "feels badly".

Rule 2 · A noun needs an adjective, not a noun, verb or adverb Q ×8

Rule: before (or right after) a noun, use the adjective form of the word: recent, strong, diplomatic, teary. Check the ending: -ic, -al, -ful, -ous, -ive, -y are usual adjective endings.

✗ It was a success attempt. → ✓ It was a successful attempt.

Rule 3 · -ing adjective vs -ed adjective Q ×4

Rule: an -ing adjective gives the cause or the doer (an interesting film makes you feel interest); an -ed adjective shows the result or the receiver (an interested viewer feels it).

✗ The lecture was bored. → ✓ The lecture was boring; the students were bored.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Trick: "-ing = jo karta hai, -ed = jis par hota hai." A horrifying man scares others; a horrified man is scared.

Rule 5 · Fixed forms: hard / hardly, late / later, fast, laden with Q ×6

Rule: some words keep one form as both adjective and adverb (fast, hard, late); adding -ly changes the meaning or makes a non-word. Some adjectives also go with fixed prepositions.

✗ He tried hardly and passed. → ✓ He tried hard and passed. (hardly = almost not)

Rule 6 · Asleep, ablaze, alive, afraid: only after the verb Q ×2

Rule: most adjectives starting with "a-" (asleep, awake, alive, alone, afraid, ablaze, aware, alike) and also ill and well (health) are used only after a linking verb, not before a noun.

✗ an afraid child → ✓ a child who was afraid / a frightened child CGL+

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Rule 7 · Some adjectives go after the noun Q ×6

Rule: an adjective that carries its own phrase (worth seeing, true to his word, dear to us) comes after the noun. "Responsible" changes meaning with position: responsible people = dependable; the people responsible = the ones to blame.

✗ It is a worth reading book. → ✓ It is a book worth reading.

CGL+ Fixed post-noun phrases: the Governor General, the heir apparent, the court martial, the sum total, the President elect, God Almighty, from time immemorial.

Rule 8 · Word order before a noun Q ×6

Rule: before a noun the order is: determiner (all, both, the, my) → ordinal (first, last) → number (three) → opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → noun.

✗ a wooden old chair → ✓ an old wooden chair

Rule 9 · Number + noun before another noun stays singular Q ×4

Rule: when "number + noun" works as an adjective before a noun, the noun has no -s and often takes a hyphen: a ten-rupee note, a five-year plan. As a plain amount it is plural: five thousand rupees.

✗ a two-years course → ✓ a two-year course

Rule 10 · The + adjective = all such people → plural Q ×3

Rule: the poor, the rich, the blind, the needy mean "all poor / blind / needy people"; they take a plural verb and never take -s. Nationality words in -ese / -ish / -ch work the same way: the Japanese.

✗ The rich does not always help the poors. → ✓ The rich do not always help the poor.

How much? The quantity scale

Rule 11 · Few / a few / the few · little / a little / the little Q ×9

Rule: few / a few / the few go with countable nouns; little / a little / the little with uncountable nouns. Without "a" the meaning is negative (almost none); with "a" it is positive (some); with "the" it means all of that small amount.

✗ Little effort from you will help us a lot. → ✓ A little effort from you will help us a lot.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Trick: "a" = aashaa (hope, positive). He has a few friends = kuch dost hain. He has few friends = lagbhag koi nahi.

Rule 12 · Less vs fewer Q ×4

Rule: fewer (comparative of few) goes with plural countable nouns; less (comparative of little) goes with uncountable nouns. So "no fewer than twenty buses", but "no less than ₹300" (a sum).

✗ Less students came today. → ✓ Fewer students came today.

Rule 13 · Some / any, much / many, a lot of, enough Q ×11

Rule: many + plural countable, much + uncountable (mostly in negatives and questions). Some in positive sentences, any in negatives, questions and after "without". "A lot of" and "a good deal of" need the "a". Several + plural noun.

✗ I don't have some money. → ✓ I don't have any money.

Rule 14 · Each of, any of: "every of" and "either of four" are wrong Q ×2

Rule: every never takes "of" (use each of / every one of). Either and neither are for two things; for three or more use any / none.

✗ Either of the three roads leads home. → ✓ Any of the three roads leads home.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

CGL+ Each = one by one, from two or more; every = all of a group of three or more, seen together: "Every student has a book."

Rule 15 · Words that look like adjectives but are pronouns Q ×2

Rule: "the same" and "such" are adjectives in good English; to stand in for a noun, use a pronoun (it, them). "Whose" is mainly for people; for things the key prefers "of which".

✗ I have read your letter and will reply to the same. → ✓ …and will reply to it. CGL+

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Check yourself

"The soup smells ___." good or well?

good. "Smells" is a linking verb here, so an adjective follows (Rule 1).

"There were ___ mistakes in his essay, so he got full marks." few or a few?

few. Full marks means almost no mistakes: the negative "few" (Rule 11).

"Twenty kilometres is no ___ than a short ride for him." less or fewer?

less. Twenty kilometres here is one distance, an amount (Rules 9 and 12).

Fix the order: "a cotton white beautiful shirt".

a beautiful white cotton shirt: opinion → colour → material (Rule 8).

"The firm needs an ___ judge, one who takes no side." uninterested or disinterested?

disinterested = neutral (Rule 4).

Adjectives (2): Degrees of Comparison

Positive, comparative, superlative · most tested: as … as, any other, than vs to, absolute adjectives, former / latter, farther / further
Three degrees in one line Positive describes one thing (tall). Comparative compares two (taller than). Superlative picks the top one from three or more (the tallest of all). Most errors come from using the wrong one of these three, or doubling them.

The degree ladder

Rule 16 · No comparison → positive degree Q ×3

Rule: if nothing is being compared, use the plain (positive) form. "Enough" and "near" go with the positive degree.

✗ He is stronger enough to lift it. → ✓ He is strong enough to lift it.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
8Ramesh is smarter enough to get selected…Ramesh is smart enough…
124…on a table nearer this window in the dining room.…on a table near this window… (no comparison)
56The photograph taken by Rahul is comparatively better than this one.…is comparatively good… (key; see note)

Rule 17 · "Than" needs a comparative Q ×5

Rule: whenever "than" appears (or a choice between two is clear), use -er or more + adjective. Compare like with like: "those in other parts", not "other parts".

✗ This road is wide than that one. → ✓ This road is wider than that one.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

67—…to follow a speech more notable for its volume than for any sense. (no improvement: two things compared)

Rule 18 · Top of a group → "the" + superlative Q ×7

Rule: when one thing is compared with all others (of all, among, in the world, ever, till now), use the + -est / most. "One of the" + superlative + plural noun.

✗ He is the better player in the team. → ✓ He is the best player in the team.

Rule 19 · Boosters: much / far + comparative, by far the + superlative Q ×4

Rule: very goes with the positive (very good); much, far, a bit go with the comparative (much better); by far the / much the go with the superlative (by far the best). "Quite" is placed before "a": quite a cheap place.

✗ This is very better. → ✓ This is much better. · ✓ This is by far the best.

Rule 20 · Never double the degree Q ×2

Rule: use either -er / -est or more / most, never both.

✗ She is more cleverer than her brother. → ✓ She is cleverer than her brother.

Rule 21 · Absolute adjectives have no degree Q ×4

Rule: some adjectives are already complete: unique, perfect, ideal, eternal, supreme, entire, round, circular, complete, right, impossible, excellent, chief, preferable. Do not add more / most / -er to them.

✗ This is the most perfect answer. → ✓ This is a perfect answer.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right

Rule 22 · Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, prefer → "to", not "than" Q ×3

Rule: Latin comparatives ending in -ior (superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, anterior, posterior), and prefer / preferable, take "to". "To" must have an object after it.

✗ He is senior than me. → ✓ He is senior to me.

Rule 23 · "Than any other" vs "of all": the "other" test Q ×5

Rule: with a comparative, if the thing belongs to the group it is compared with, add "other" (any other metal). If it is outside the group, do not add "other". With a superlative ("of all"), never add "other".

One fact, three degrees CGL+ Superlative: Mumbai is the largest city in India.
Comparative: Mumbai is larger than any other city in India.
Positive: No other city in India is as large as Mumbai.

Rule 24 · Two qualities of one person → "more + positive" Q ×2

Rule: when you compare two qualities of the same person or thing, do not use -er; use more + plain adjective.

✗ He is wiser than brave. → ✓ He is more wise than brave.

Rule 25 · Two adjectives joined by "and" share one degree Q ×2

Rule: adjectives joined by "and" (or "or") must be in the same degree. Each one keeps its own form.

✗ the bravest and honest soldier → ✓ the bravest and most honest soldier

Rule: "as + positive + as" is a fixed frame. In "as good as, if not better than", both parts must be complete. Use much with uncountable nouns, many with countable, well (adverb) when comparing actions. "So … as" is only for negatives. Compare the same kind of thing.

Rule 27 · Elder / eldest vs older / oldest Q ×3

Rule: elder and eldest are for members of one family and come before a noun or after "the" (my elder brother, the eldest of three). For age in general, and with "than", use older / oldest.

✗ My cousin is elder than me. → ✓ My cousin is older than me.

Rule 28 · Former / latter for two; first / last for three or more; later ≠ latter Q ×5

Rule: former (the first of two) and latter (the second of two) are only for two items. For three or more say the first / the last. "Later" is about time (afterwards).

✗ Of tea, coffee and milk, I like the latter. → ✓ …I like the last.

Rule 29 · Farther (distance) vs further (more, additional) Q ×3

Rule: farther / farthest = physical distance; further / furthest = more, extra, additional (further details, further delay, further notice).

✗ Until farther notice, the office is closed. → ✓ Until further notice…
