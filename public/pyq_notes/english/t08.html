English Grammar · Rule book · 70 of 150 questions

Conjunction (1): Pairs That Must Match

Error Pro bank, 150 questions · most tested: no sooner–than, hardly–when, neither–nor, such–as / such–that, too–to, than vs then, as…as
How to read this rule book A conjunction is a joining word: it joins two words or two clauses (a clause = a group of words with its own subject and verb). Every rule below has one plain line, one fresh example, and a table built from the bank (Q = question number). Highlighted = the wrong part. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. CGL+ = standard SSC rule or example not in this bank.

The partner chart

Many joining words come in fixed pairs. If you see the first word, the second one is already decided. Learn this chart and you can solve about half of the bank in seconds.

Trick: "No sooner ka dost than, Hardly ka dost when." Say it twice: no-sooner-THAN, hardly-WHEN. Both start with "had" or "did" right after them.

Rule 1 · No sooner … than Q ×5

Rule: "no sooner" (= just after) always pairs with than, never "then" or "when". The helping verb comes before the subject: No sooner had / did + subject + verb … than.

✗ No sooner had the guests left then it began to rain. → ✓ No sooner had the guests left than it began to rain.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
7No sooner did the child start crying then the mother hugged him.…than the mother hugged him.
13No sooner did Priya get her report card when she started jumping.…than she started jumping.
97No sooner did she send her application then she received a reply.…than she received a reply.
80Just when I sit down, than the doorbell rang.No sooner did I sit down than the doorbell rang. ("than" is already there, so the opener must be "no sooner")
144Just when we locked the door and stepped outside, than it started raining.No sooner did we lock the main door and step outside than it started raining.

CGL+ Two correct forms: "No sooner had he reached than…" (had + V3) and "No sooner did he reach than…" (did + V1). Never "No sooner he reached".

Rule 2 · Hardly / Scarcely … when Q ×4

Rule: "hardly" and "scarcely" (= just, barely) pair with when (or "before"), never "than". After "had" use the third form (V3).

✗ Scarcely had we started the match than the floodlights failed. → ✓ Scarcely had we started the match when the floodlights failed.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
11They had hardly completed half of the work than the boss called them.…when the boss called them.
101Hardly had the students begun solving the questions than the invigilator announced……when the invigilator announced…
90Hardly had he sit on the chair than it broke.Hardly had he sat on the chair when it broke. (had + V3, and "when")
150Hardly had she opened the classroom window than a gust blew inside.…opened the classroom window when a gust blew inside.

Rule 3 · As soon as: full form, no "then", normal word order Q ×5

Rule: "as soon as" is one joining word of three parts. It already joins the two clauses, so do not add "then". If the opener has no "than / when" later and no flipped verb, "as soon as" is the right choice, not "no sooner" or "hardly".

✗ As soon as the bell rang, then the children ran out. → ✓ As soon as the bell rang, the children ran out.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
4As soon as he opened the old wooden box then a rat jumped out.…box, a rat jumped out. (remove "then")
94As soon as the heavy rain started pouring, then people rushed to take shelter.…people rushed… (remove "then")
25As soon he heard the sound of thunder, he hid.As soon as he heard…
79No sooner I get the message, I shall pass it on.As soon as I get the message… (no "than", no inversion)
143No sooner you complete the application, you will receive an email.As soon as you complete…

Rule 4 · Both … and Q ×2

Rule: "both" pairs only with and. "Along with" and "as well as" cannot replace it.

✗ Both the coach as well as the players were praised. → ✓ Both the coach and the players were praised.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
2Both the rich along with the poor are responsible…Both the rich and the poor…
92Both government policies as well as individual responsibility are necessary…Both government policies and individual responsibility…

CGL+ "Both" means two, so do not use it with a negative: ✗ Both of them did not come → ✓ Neither of them came.

Rule 5 · Either … or; neither … nor Q ×5

Rule: either goes with or; neither goes with nor. Never mix them, and never use "and" in place of the second word.

✗ He drinks neither tea or coffee. → ✓ He drinks neither tea nor coffee.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
32Miss Marple is neither a good singer or the good stage artist.…nor a good stage artist.
69He is interested neither in business or on the job.…neither in business nor in a job.
116She is neither interested in casual relationships or ready for marriage.…nor ready for marriage.
61Either you and he will have to pay.Either you or he will have to pay.
134Either this electric car … and the hybrid SUV will be selected.…or the hybrid SUV…

CGL+ "Neither" is already negative: ✗ He did not take neither tea nor coffee → ✓ He took neither tea nor coffee. The verb agrees with the nearer subject: "Either you or he is…"

Rule 6 · Not only … but also (and the flip when it comes first) Q ×4

Rule: "not only" pairs with but also, never "and also" or "but even … also". When a sentence starts with "Not only", flip the verb like a question: Not only do / does / did + subject + verb.

✗ Not only she sings, but she also dances. → ✓ Not only does she sing, but she also dances.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
51…is not only beautiful but even intelligent also.…not only beautiful but also intelligent.
63…he not only gives him the information and also handles correspondence.…but also handles…
35Not only children have fewer chances of surviving…Not only do children have… (present tense, plural subject)
118Not only the employees reported repeated system crashes…Not only did the employees report… (past: did + V1)

Rule 7 · A pair must join two equal parts Q ×4

Rule: both–and, either–or, neither–nor, not only–but also must stand just before the same kind of word: verb with verb, phrase with phrase, noun with noun. Move the first word so both halves match. This is called parallelism (keeping both sides in the same shape).

✗ She not only plays the guitar but also the violin. → ✓ She plays not only the guitar but also the violin. (noun || noun)

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
10The library is not only equipped with good books but also with journals.…is equipped not only with good books but also with journals. (with… || with…)
55We have not only come to meet you but also to congratulate you.We have come not only to meet you but also to congratulate you. (to… || to…)
100The campaign raised not only awareness … but also encouraged people…The campaign not only raised awareness … but also encouraged… (verb || verb)
133The policy neither aims to reduce unemployment nor improve education.The policy aims neither to reduce unemployment nor (to) improve… (to-verb || to-verb)
Trick: Look at what comes right after "but also / nor / or". Put the same type of word right after "not only / neither / either". "Dono taraf ek jaisa maal."

Rule 8 · Whether … or (not "if … or") Q ×2

Rule: when two choices are given with "or" (or "or not"), start with whether, not "if".

✗ I will go if it rains or not. → ✓ I will go whether it rains or not.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
38I shall buy this book if you like it or not.…whether you like it or not.
121…will implement strict rules if the influencers like them or not.…whether the influencers like them or not.

CGL+ After a preposition, only "whether" works: ✓ It depends on whether he agrees (✗ on if he agrees).

Rule 9 · Lest … should Q ×3

Rule: "lest" means "so that … not" (kahin aisa na ho ki). It takes should + V1 (or plain V1). Never add "not", "may" or "will" after it.

✗ Speak softly lest the baby will wake up. → ✓ Speak softly lest the baby should wake up.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
12"Walk carefully, lest you may fall down or hurt yourself."…lest you should fall down…
68Keep him at arm's length lest you may not repent.…lest you should repent. ("lest" already has the "not")
102"Complete your homework on time, lest you will face difficulty…"…lest you should face difficulty…

Rule 10 · Such … as ("jo") vs such / so … that ("ki") Q ×8

Rule: after "such", use as when the next part means "who / which / like" (jo, jaisa); use that when it shows a result (ki). "So + adjective" takes that for a result. "But", "who" and "which" are wrong partners.

✓ Choose such friends as are loyal. (jo) · ✓ It was such a hot day that we stayed in. (ki)

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
26Such individuals who take up this role often find life frustrating.Such individuals as take up this role…
112Such intelligence officers who monitor dark-web activities…Such … officers as monitor…
54He is not such a clever boy that you are.…such a clever boy as you are. (jaisa)
39He gave such a long speech but everybody felt bored.…such a long speech that everybody felt bored. (ki)
122The storm was such a violent one but fishermen couldn't sail.…that fishermen couldn't sail.
74—Such was his performance that the audience gave a standing ovation. (no substitution)
140—Such was the intensity of the storm that the town was evacuated. (no substitution)
81She had grown too thin which she looked like a scarecrow.She had grown so thin that she looked like a scarecrow.
Trick: Such + noun, so + adjective. "Such a boy", "so clever". Both take "that" for a result.

Rule 11 · Too … to; … enough to Q ×6

Rule: "too + adjective + to + V1" gives a negative idea (itna … ki nahi). "Adjective + enough to + V1" gives a positive idea. "So" and "such" cannot take "to + V1", and "too" cannot take "for + verb".

✗ The box is so heavy to lift. → ✓ The box is too heavy to lift. · ✓ He is strong enough to lift it.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
27He is too immature for handle the situation.…too immature to handle…
113The witness was too nervous for speak the truth.…too nervous to speak…
42Aren't you such young to travel by yourself?Aren't you too young (or young enough) to travel…
124You are so late to start preparing for this year's exam.You are too late to start…
87She was so tall to reach the top shelf.She was tall enough to reach… (she could reach it)
147The ladder was so long to reach the roof.The ladder was long enough to reach…

CGL+ "Enough" comes after the adjective but before the noun: old enough, enough money.

Rule 12 · Than (comparison) vs then (time); other … than Q ×4

Rule: after a comparative (easier, better, more) and after "other / rather", use than. "Then" only means "after that".

✗ Prevention is better then cure. → ✓ Prevention is better than cure.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
47It is easier to preach then to follow.…than to follow.
128Students find it much easier to watch lectures then to attend a live class.…than to attend…
3He has no other option but to work hard.…no other option than to work hard. (key: "other" → than)
93…he has no other option but to diversify his investments……than to diversify…

Rule 13 · As … as, so … as, the same as Q ×8

Rule: to say "equal", use as + adjective + as. In a negative sentence, "so … as" is also fine. Never "as … like", "so … like" or "same like". In "as good as, if not better than", both partners must be written in full.

✗ My phone is as fast like yours. → ✓ My phone is as fast as yours.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
8She is as intelligent, if not more intelligent than one of the students.She is as intelligent as, if not more intelligent than, …
98The new technology is as efficient, if not more than most systems.…as efficient as, if not more than, …
67She is as good if not better than her sister.She is as good as, if not better than, her sister.
22Many of the founding fathers are so revered as Ambedkar, if not more.…are as revered as Ambedkar… (positive sentence → as…as)
72He's not so friendly like she is.He's not so friendly as she is.
138This phone is not as durable like the previous model.…not so durable as the previous model.
15…computers can think same like human beings.…think the same as human beings.
104…AI systems can behave same like the human brain.…behave the same as the human brain.

Rule 14 · One joining word is enough Q ×8

Rule: two clauses need only one joining word. Do not stack two that mean the same thing: "as well as also", "supposing if", "the reason … because", "asked that why", "know that if".

✗ Although he worked hard, but he failed. → ✓ Although he worked hard, he failed. CGL+

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
19It will save you time as well as also money.…time as well as money. (drop "also")
16Supposing if a number of ghosts meet us in the forest…Supposing (or If) a number of ghosts…
36The reason for visiting Meerut was because my mother needed care.The reason … was that… ("reason" already means "because")
119The reason for aspirants gathering … was because the results were delayed.…was that the results…
6He asked me that why I was not preparing…He asked me why… (the wh-word itself joins)
96She asked me that why I had suddenly moved…She asked me why…
117The students asked the administrator that if the portal would crash……asked the administrator if the portal…
127I was amazed to know that if Riya had designed a working model……amazed to know that Riya had designed… (drop "if")

Rule 15 · That = "ki"; if / whether = "kya" Q ×2

Rule: to report a fact, use that (ki). To report a yes/no question, use if / whether (kya). "As" means "because", so it cannot do either job.

✗ She asked me that I liked tea. → ✓ She asked me if I liked tea. · ✓ She told me that she liked tea.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
33Mukesh asked the conductor that he knew where the museum was.…asked the conductor if he knew… (a question: kya)
45I was surprised to see as Avika could write such good poems.…surprised to see that Avika could write… (ki)

Rule 16 · More fixed pairs SSC asks CGL+

Rule: these pairs are not in the bank but appear often in SSC error-spotting.

Pair✗ Wrong✓ Right
though / although … yet (or nothing)Though he is poor, but he is honest.Though he is poor, yet he is honest. (or drop "but")
between … andbetween you or mebetween you and me
from … tofrom Monday till Friday (formal SSC style)from Monday to Friday
rather … than / would rather … thanI would rather die but beg.I would rather die than beg.
prefer … toI prefer tea than coffee.I prefer tea to coffee.
no less … thanHe is no less clever as his brother.He is no less clever than his brother.
so that … may / mightHe works hard so that he will pass.…so that he may pass. (might in the past)

Check yourself

"Scarcely had he left the room ___ the phone rang." than or when?

when. Hardly / scarcely pair with "when" (Rule 2).

"He not only lost his money but also his health." Where is the error?

"not only lost" should be "lost not only": money and health are both nouns, so put "not only" just before "his money" (Rule 7).

"Neither Ravi or Mohan came." Fix it.

Neither Ravi nor Mohan came (Rule 5).

"The water is so cold to drink." Fix it.

The water is too cold to drink (Rule 11).

"The reason he failed is because he did not study." Fix it.

The reason he failed is that he did not study (Rule 14).

English Grammar · Rule book · 80 of 150 questions

Conjunction (2): Pick the Word by Meaning

unless / until, if vs although, but vs while, otherwise, so that, because vs because of, in spite of, "no choice but to"

In these questions the grammar is often fine; the meaning is wrong. First say the sentence in Hindi in your head (agar? haalanki? warna? taaki?), then pick the English word that carries that meaning.

Meaning first: the linker finder

Rule 17 · Unless / until already contain "not" Q ×7

Rule: unless = "if … not" (agar nahi); until = "till … not" (jab tak nahi). The clause after them must stay positive: no "not", "doesn't" or "don't".

✗ You will miss the train unless you don't hurry. → ✓ You will miss the train unless you hurry.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
1One can never imagine how hot it gets in Delhi unless one doesn't go there.…unless one goes there.
91A woman cannot understand … unless she doesn't experience the sleepless nights.…unless she experiences…
76Unless we are not sure of our goals, we cannot achieve them.Unless we are sure of our goals…
82I cannot help you if you tell me your problem.…unless you tell me… (not "unless you don't tell")
46—Unless you prepare well, you will not get a first class. (no error)
83—Unless someone makes a complaint, they will not clear the garbage. (no substitution: without / despite / except are not joining words)
145—Unless residents submit a written request, they will not install streetlights. (no substitution)

Rule 18 · Use unless / until when the meaning is "agar nahi / jab tak nahi" Q ×4

Rule: if the sentence says "X will not happen if Y does not happen", the joining word is unless. If it says "keep doing X till Y happens", it is until.

✗ You cannot drive although you have a licence. → ✓ You cannot drive unless you have a licence.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
29You cannot lead a healthy life although you stop smoking.…unless you stop smoking.
108You will not succeed in this exam as you study regularly.…unless you study regularly.
114You cannot achieve financial stability although you save every month.…unless you save…
71…walk down the corridor then you came to the main staircase.…walk down the corridor until you come to the staircase.

Rule 19 · Condition words: if, provided, only if, as long as Q ×10

Rule: for "agar" use if or provided (that); for "sirf agar / tabhi" use only if; for "jab tak (ki)" use as long as. "As such" (= as it is), "but if", "only then" and "so much as" cannot start a condition.

✗ You can borrow my bike so much as you return it today. → ✓ You can borrow my bike as long as (or provided) you return it today.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
75Unless you carry a Covid-19 negative report, you can travel by air.Provided you carry a negative report, you can travel…
141Unless you have balance in your metro card, you will face no problem.Provided you have balance…
57Our nation can make significant growth but if we all work collectively.…only if we all work…
131Our rural healthcare system can improve but if we invest more.…only if we invest more…
70You can have a comfortable journey only then you are getting your seats reserved.…only if you get your seats reserved…
89So much as you behave like this, the decision cannot be revoked.As long as you behave like this…
149So much as you keep helping me, I will not tell your secret.As long as you keep helping me…
34She will come to work though it doesn't rain tomorrow.…if it doesn't rain tomorrow.
43As such you need any money, just write to me.If you need any money…
125As such you ever receive suspicious messages, report and block the sender.If you ever receive…

CGL+ "As such" is an adverb meaning "in that role / as it is": He is a guest, and as such we must respect him. It never means "if".

Rule 20 · Although / though for "haalanki" Q ×8

Rule: when the second fact is surprising after the first (haalanki … phir bhi), join them with although / though / even though. "Even" alone is an adverb, not a joining word; "as, because, if, unless, until, yet" give the wrong meaning.

✗ He came to school even he had fever. → ✓ He came to school even though he had fever.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
9Alex picked up the boxes quite easily even they were heavy.…though they were heavy.
99Maria finished the marathon with a smile even she was exhausted.…although / even though she was exhausted.
17Until I was still a little weak, I decided to walk home.Although I was still a little weak…
41Zoya won the first prize in the race unless she stumbled and fell.…although she stumbled and fell.
49If they said nothing, she could sense their disapproval.Although they said nothing…
84Because she didn't love him, she had to marry him.Although she didn't love him…
105As she had prepared well, she forgot her lines on stage.Although she had prepared well…
31The construction is going on yet the airport authority has objected.…going on though / although the authority has objected.

Rule 21 · But / while for "lekin / jabki" Q ×9

Rule: two opposite ideas are joined by but. Two people or things compared side by side ("A does this, jabki B does that") take while / whereas. "And", "so", "as", "if" and "even" do not show contrast.

✗ Ravi likes cricket, even his brother likes chess. → ✓ Ravi likes cricket, while his brother likes chess.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
28We were tired and happy because our efforts did not go in vain.We were tired but happy…
106We were exhausted and delighted that we had reached the summit.We were exhausted but delighted…
30Smartphone cameras take near-professional pictures, so they also have limitations.…but they also have limitations.
109Smart home devices made life convenient, so they also pose privacy concerns.…but they also pose…
115A career gives you security and respect, as it also demands sacrifices.…but it also demands…
24If he is very good at mathematics, his brother is hopeless.While he is very good at maths, his brother…
111If this cadet can run ten miles with ease, his partner struggles…While this cadet can run ten miles…
78Cakes are Jaya's favourite snack, even samosas is Aparna's choice.…while samosas are Aparna's choice.
142Pasta is Rohan's favourite food, even burgers is Priya's favourite.…while burgers are Priya's favourite.

Rule 22 · And when there is no contrast Q ×2

Rule: if both ideas point the same way (both good, both bad), join them with and, not "but". Keep both verbs in the same form.

✗ She is kind but helpful. → ✓ She is kind and helpful. (two good qualities, no contrast)

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
86She was smiling but looking pleased about something.She was smiling and looked pleased…
146She spoke politely but behaving confidently during the meeting.She spoke politely and behaved confidently… (spoke || behaved)

Rule 23 · Otherwise / else for "warna" Q ×7

Rule: after an order or advice, the bad result that follows "if you don't" is joined with otherwise / else / or. "But", "and", "however", "unless" and "in case" give the wrong meaning.

✗ Wear a mask, however you may fall ill. → ✓ Wear a mask, otherwise you may fall ill.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
44It's raining, so you should take an umbrella, but you will get wet.…otherwise you will get wet.
48Do not let the opportunity slip away and you will repent.…otherwise you will repent.
50Put off the air conditioner unless you will catch a cold.…otherwise you will catch a cold.
66Keep the dog tied indoors; however, it may bite some stranger.…; else it may bite…
77Hurry up, in case you will be late for school.Hurry up, otherwise you will be late.
126Always wear a helmet but you might face a hefty fine.…otherwise / or you might face…
137Ensure your car has fuel … however you may get stranded.…otherwise you may get stranded.

CGL+ Two classic SSC lines: Work hard, or you will fail. · Walk fast lest you should miss the bus. (Rule 9)

Rule 24 · Time: when (a moment), while (a stretch), during (+ noun) Q ×7

Rule: when is for a quick event (saw, heard, reached); while is for something going on for a time (was raining, were cooking); during is a preposition and takes only a noun (during the holiday), never a clause. "Although" is wrong where the meaning is just "jab".

✗ He slept during the teacher was speaking. → ✓ He slept while the teacher was speaking. · ✓ He slept during the lecture.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
5We stayed in Jim's flat during he was on holiday.…while he was on holiday.
95The children were playing during their mothers were preparing lunch.…while their mothers were preparing lunch.
62While he saw the bear, he ran to the nearest tree.When he saw the bear… (seeing is a moment)
135While he heard the explosion, he rushed outside.When he heard the explosion…
59Cyclone Idai killed 157 people although it tore across Southern Africa.…as / when it tore across…
110The earthquake caused massive destruction although it struck the city……when it struck the city…
64Last before year, Shirley got very good grades.The year before last, Shirley got… (fixed time phrase = two years ago)

Rule 25 · Purpose: so that + clause; so as to + V1; in order that ≠ because Q ×7

Rule: for "taaki", use so that + subject + may/can (a clause), or so as to / in order to + V1. "That" alone and "so then" are wrong. And "in order that" means purpose, so it cannot give a reason (kyunki).

✗ He ran fast so that to catch the bus. → ✓ He ran fast so as to catch the bus. · ✓ He ran fast so that he could catch the bus.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
14I work hard and study well that I may pass……so that I may pass…
103He saves a portion of his income that he can build a secure future.…so that he can build…
23The farmers are using HYV seeds so then they can get a better yield.…so that they can get…
37He shouted with all his might so that to catch someone's attention.…so as to catch…
120He waved his hands so that to alert the pedestrians.…so as to alert…
60She got two quick promotions in order that she has good skills.…because she has good communication skills.
132Army units have been deployed in order that agencies have noticed suspicious activity.…because intelligence agencies have noticed…

Rule 26 · Reason: because + clause; because of / due to / owing to + noun Q ×7

Rule: "because, as, since" join a full clause (subject + verb). "Because of, due to, owing to" are prepositions and take only a noun. "In case (of)" means "if it happens", not "because"; "notwithstanding" means "in spite of", not "because".

✗ The match was stopped due to it was raining. → ✓ …because it was raining. · ✓ …due to the rain.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
52Preeti is absent from the meeting due to she is out of station.…because she is out of station.
129She missed her practical due to she was suffering from medical issues.…because she was suffering…
123The train was delayed because the dense fog covering the tracks.…because of the dense fog… (no verb follows)
85The train was delayed in order to an accident.…owing to an accident.
40She lost a big order in case of her own carelessness.…because of her own carelessness.
58We had to decline several orders in case that the production was held up.…because the production was held up.
21Notwithstanding we were all busy that weekend, we had to cancel the outing.Since / As we were all busy… (a reason)

Rule 27 · In spite of / despite + noun; although + clause Q ×3

Rule: "in spite of" and "despite" are prepositions: they take a noun or -ing. Before a full clause use although / though. "Nevertheless" and "however" are adverbs and cannot join two clauses with only a comma.

✗ In spite of it was late, they kept working. → ✓ Although it was late… · ✓ In spite of the late hour…

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
56Inspite Smita had taken part in many serials, this was her first live show.Although Smita had taken part… ("in spite" is also two words and needs "of")
73In spite of my niece's dog Casper is very mischievous, he is lovable.Although my niece's dog Casper is…
139In spite of my friend's puppy gets excited and chews everything, everyone adores him.Although my friend's puppy gets excited…

Rule 28 · Examples: such as / like, not "as" Q ×3

Rule: to give examples (jaise ki), use such as or like. "As" alone cannot introduce a list of examples.

✗ I enjoy sports as tennis and swimming. → ✓ I enjoy sports such as tennis and swimming.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
18…have brought some fire crackers as lauki, rockets, etc.…crackers such as / like lauki, rockets…
20…you must take fruit as apples, oranges, mangoes……fruit such as apples, oranges…
107…by releasing natural chemicals as pheromones, essential oils……chemicals such as pheromones…

CGL+ Do not add "etc." after "such as": the list is already open. ✗ such as apples, mangoes, etc. → ✓ such as apples and mangoes.

Rule 29 · Fixed "but" phrases: no choice but to, but for Q ×6

Rule: "no choice / no alternative but to + V1" means "only one option". "But for + noun" means "without", and is followed by "would have + V3".

✗ I had no choice to agree. → ✓ I had no choice but to agree. · ✓ But for your help, I would have failed.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
53The soldiers had no choice when to obey the commander.…no choice but to obey…
130The doctors had no choice when to perform an emergency surgery.…no choice but to perform…
65The soldiers had no choice to report the loss.…no choice but to report…
136The family had no choice to wait for the towing service.…no choice but to wait…
88—But for their valour, they would have been defeated. (no improvement)
148—But for his timely medical advice, the patient would have suffered… (no improvement)

Rule 30 · More meaning traps SSC asks CGL+

Rule: standard pairs from SSC error-spotting that this bank does not test.

Point✗ Wrong✓ Right
because … so (double reason)Because he was ill, so he stayed home.Because he was ill, he stayed home.

English Grammar · Rule book · 40 of 80 questions

Types of Clauses: Noun, Adjective, Adverb

Error Pro bank, 80 questions (Q1–40 here) · most tested: the same word (when, where, who, whatever) doing three different jobs
How to read this rule book A clause is a group of words with its own subject and verb. A main clause can stand alone ("I know the boy"). A dependent clause cannot ("who came here"); it works like one word inside the main clause. If it works like a noun, it is a noun clause; like an adjective, an adjective clause; like an adverb, an adverb clause. In the tables, highlighted = the clause the question asks about; Q = question number; Q ×N = how many bank questions test the rule.

The three-question test

Trick: "Verb se what? = noun clause. Noun ke baad kaun sa? = adjective clause. Verb se kab, kahan, kyun, kaise, kis shart par? = adverb clause."

Rule 1 · Noun clause: the object of the verb Q ×12

Rule: after verbs like know, ask, tell, say, wonder, a clause starting with that, if, whether, who, whom, whose, which, what, when, where, why, how often answers "what?". It is the object, so it is a noun clause, even when it starts with "when" or "where".

✗ I wonder where she has gone = adverb clause → ✓ noun clause ("I wonder what?" → where she has gone).

Rule 2 · Whatever / whoever: noun or adverb? Q ×3

Rule: if the -ever clause is the object of a verb or preposition (it answers "what?" or "to whom?"), it is a noun clause. If the sentence already has its object and the -ever clause means "no matter what" (chahe kuch bhi), it is an adverb clause.

Rule 3 · Adjective clause: who / whom / whose / which / that after a noun Q ×7

Rule: a clause that comes right after a noun and tells "which one?" is an adjective clause (also called a relative clause). Its joining word (who, whom, whose, which, that, or "as" after "such") points back to that noun.

✗ I met the girl who won the prize = noun clause → ✓ adjective clause (which girl? the one who won).

Rule 4 · When / where / why: after a noun = adjective; no noun = adverb Q ×4

Types of Sentences: Simple, Compound, Complex, Compound-Complex

Error Pro bank, Q41–80 · count the clauses, then look at the joining word

To name a sentence, do two things: count the clauses (each needs its own subject + verb), then look at the joining word. That's it.

The building blocks

Rule 7 · Simple sentence: one subject–verb unit Q ×9

Rule: a simple sentence has one clause. Long phrases (in the park, after the match) do not add clauses. Two verbs sharing one subject ("looked up and smiled") are still one clause.

✗ She opened the door and walked in = compound → ✓ simple (one subject "she", two verbs).

Rule: two main clauses joined by a semicolon + however / therefore / moreover / nevertheless / consequently + comma also make a compound sentence. These words are adverbs, not subordinators, so no clause becomes dependent.

Rule 10 · Complex sentence: one main + one or more dependent clauses Q ×8

Rule: if the clauses are joined only by subordinators (because, if, since, until, when, although, that, what, who…), the sentence is complex, however many dependent clauses it has. No FANBOYS between main clauses.
