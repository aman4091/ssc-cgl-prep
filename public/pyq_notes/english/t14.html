Rule 1 · Find the head noun, then match the verb PYQ ×11

Rule: The verb agrees with the main noun of the subject, not with the nearest noun. Cut away "of…" phrases, commas and "who/which" clauses first.

✗ The list of items are on the desk. → ✓ The list of items is on the desk.

Rule 3 · After a modal, do / did, or "to" → base verb (V1) PYQ ×9

Rule: can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must + V1. do / does / did + V1. to + V1. Never add -s, -ed or another "to".

Rule 6 · Inversion: negative or "only" opener → did / had + subject PYQ ×3

Rule 9 · Who or whom? Object needs whom PYQ ×3

Rule: If the relative pronoun is the object (someone else does the action to the person), use whom; after a preposition always whom.

✗ The man who I met was kind. → ✓ The man whom I met was kind.

Rule 12 · Articles: a / an / the / none PYQ ×3

Rule 13 · Comparison: senior / superior TO, more of two, most of many PYQ ×9

Rule: Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, prefer) take to, never than, never "more". Between two → -er / more; among three or more → the -est / the most.

Para Jumbles (1): The Method

Steno 2026 · 9–15 Sep · four kinds: 4-sentence paragraphs (45), sentence-part jumbles (52), fixed-ends P-Q-R-S (6), word groups (3)
What Steno 2026 askedEvery one of the 106 items is listed in the next two chapters with its key and the one clue that cracks it. This chapter teaches the method once. Topics were mostly science and tech (AI, blockchain, desalination, Hyperloop) and Indian everyday life (Republic Day, Sikkim farming, Kerala coir).

Six steps

Hooks: pronouns and connectors

Think of each sentence as a train coach with a hook at the front. The hook is a word that needs something before it.

Hook wordWhat it needs before itUsual placeSteno 2026 example
This / These / Such + nounthe noun or idea just named2nd or 3rd"This field", "This trend", "These apps" PYQ
He / She / It / They / Its / Theirthe person or thing namednever 1st"He checks blood pressure", "It records transactions" PYQ
Moreover / Also / Furthera point of the same kindmiddle"Moreover, advances in battery technology" PYQ
As a result / Consequently / Thereforethe cause3rd or 4th"As a result, trust in the brand declined" PYQ
However / Buta positive point to contrastusually last"However, experts caution…" PYQ
Finally / Soon / Eventually / In the futurethe earlier stepslast"Soon, there was a heavy shower" PYQ
Once X / Then / Building on thisstep Xmiddle"Once features are finalized" PYQ
Trick: "Pehla vaakya naam leta hai, baaki uski taraf ishaara karte hain." The first sentence names the topic; every later sentence points back with this / it / they.

Opening-sentence clues

  • Definition: "X is a…", "X refers to…", "X is the underlying technology…" (synthetic biology, digital twin, biohacking, blockchain, Hyperloop).
  • Named subject + setting: "For more than a millennium, the Maya civilisation thrived…", "The Delhi Book Fair features…".
  • Never an opener: a sentence starting with a pronoun, "This", "However", "As a result", "Moreover", "Therefore", or "Finally".

Chronology and cause–effect

  • Process: collect → log → analyse → use (eDNA, Q122 · 10 Sep, S1); clean data → features → train → test (Q121 · 10 Sep, S1).
  • Cause → effect: quality checks ignored → defects → trust fell → sales fell (Q187 · 11 Sep, S2).
  • Event steps: breach → vulnerabilities found → security strengthened → trust restored (Q184 · 12 Sep, S1).

CGL+ SSC also sets "find the odd sentence" and "fill the missing middle sentence" items. The same hooks work: the odd sentence has no hook into the chain.

Check yourself

Which sentence opens: (a) "However, costs remain high." (b) "Solar power is energy from the sun."

(b). It defines the topic and has no connector.

"These apps offer…" can it be the first sentence?

No. "These" points back to a noun named earlier.

Where does "As a result, …" usually go?

After the cause, in the middle or near the end.

"In the future, these advances could…" opener or closer?

Closer. A future outlook ends the paragraph.

Fastest way to test the options?

Fix the opener and the closer; usually one option survives.

Check yourself

Which part opens: "a. the old man b. slowly c. The dog followed"?

c. It has the capital letter (The dog followed the old man slowly).

"had almost ___": what must follow?

A past participle (V3): "had almost finished".

Where does a time phrase like "last Friday" go?

At the end, after the place.

"not only" in the fixed part 1: what comes next?

A verb, then "but also" + a matching verb (Q197 · 12 Sep, S2).

"and dissolving" is first or last in a list?

Last. "and" joins the final item.
