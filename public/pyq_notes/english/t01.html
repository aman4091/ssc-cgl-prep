Rule 1 · Uncountable nouns never take -s Q ×12

Rule: an uncountable noun is a mass you cannot count one by one (furniture, advice, information, luggage, homework, temper). It has no plural form and takes a singular verb.

✗ The sceneries of Kashmir are lovely. → ✓ The scenery of Kashmir is lovely.

Rule 2 · No "a / an" with an uncountable noun; count it with a unit word Q ×4

Rule: do not put "a" before an uncountable noun, and do not add -s. To count it, use a unit word (a word for one portion): a piece of, a loaf of, a bar of, an item of.

✗ She gave me an advice. → ✓ She gave me a piece of advice / some advice.

Rule 3 · Some nouns are always plural in form Q ×11

Rule: savings, earnings, surroundings, means (= money, income), proceeds (= money from a sale), headquarters, and pair nouns (trousers, scissors, spectacles) always keep the -s.

Rule 4 · Nouns whose number is fixed: cattle, gentry, children, offspring, public Q ×10

Rule: cattle, gentry, police, people are already plural (no -s, plural verb). Children is already plural (no "childrens"). Offspring stays the same for one or many. Public is used as a singular whole (no "publics").

✗ The police has caught the thiefs. → ✓ The police have caught the thieves.

Rule 6 · Compound nouns: put the -s on the main noun Q ×5

Rule: in a hyphenated compound, the main noun (the person or thing) takes the -s, not the tail. If the compound has no noun inside (break-in, grown-up), add -s at the end.

✗ Both my brother-in-laws are doctors. → ✓ Both my brothers-in-law are doctors.

Rule 7 · A number above one takes a plural noun; fractions too Q ×6

Rule: after two, three, several, one and a half (anything more than one) the noun is plural. In word fractions, a numerator above one adds -s: one-third, but two-thirds.

✗ The baby is two year old. → ✓ The baby is two years old. · But: a two-year-old baby (adjective, no -s).

Rule 8 · Number + dozen / hundred / thousand: no -s Q ×3

Rule: after a definite number, dozen, hundred, thousand, million, score work like adjectives and stay singular, with no "of": two dozen eggs. Without a number, use the plural + of: dozens of eggs.

✗ Five hundreds people came. → ✓ Five hundred people came. · ✓ Hundreds of people came.

Rule 11 · The + adjective = a group of people (plural noun) Q ×2

Rule: an adjective alone cannot be a noun. Put "the" before it (the poor, the rich, the disabled) or add a noun (disabled people). It means all such people and takes a plural verb.

✗ We must help poor. → ✓ We must help the poor.

Q✗ Wrong✓ Right
8Some seats are reserved for disabled in each compartment.…for the disabled / disabled people…
88The government has announced special schemes for disabled.…for the disabled…

Never add -s to such a word: ✗ the poors, the richs.

Check yourself

"He gave me many valuable ___." advices or pieces of advice?

pieces of advice. "Advice" is uncountable and "many" needs a countable noun (Rules 1–2).

"Both his ___ are lawyers." son-in-laws or sons-in-law?

sons-in-law. The -s goes on the main noun "son" (Rule 6).

"I need three ___ bananas." dozen or dozens?

dozen. After a definite number it stays singular, without "of" (Rule 8).

"The ___ of the sale went to charity." proceed or proceeds?

proceeds. The money noun is always plural; "proceed" is the verb (Rule 3).

"The cattle ___ grazing." is or are?

are. "Cattle" is already plural (Rule 4).

Rule 14 · Show "ka / ki / ke" with 's Q ×10

Rule: when one noun owns the next noun (X ka Y), the owner takes 's. People, animals, countries, cities and heavenly bodies (earth, sun, moon) can take 's. For a long owner like "the President of India", the 's goes at the end of the whole phrase.

✗ The manager car is new. → ✓ The manager's car is new.

Rule 18 · Avoid two 's in a row; "the date of my birth" Q ×4

Rule 19 · Possessive before an -ing noun Q ×3

Rule: a gerund is a verb + -ing used as a noun (beating, scolding). When the sentence is about the action, the person doing it takes 's (or my / his / their).

Pronoun (2): Who / Which / That, Each / Either, This / Those

Relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that, what), distributives, two vs more (either / any, each other / one another), demonstratives, "those of", it vs there

Choosing the relative pronoun

A relative pronoun joins a describing clause to a noun: "the man who came", "the book that I read".

Trick: who / whom test: answer the clause with he / him. "___ do you think she is?" → "she is he" → who. "of ___ not much is known" → "of him" → whom. "He" = who, "him" = whom (both end in m).

Rule 13 · who for people, which for things Q ×5

Rule: people (and named persons) take who / whom; things, firms, animals take which. A sentence with two finite verbs needs a relative pronoun to join them.
