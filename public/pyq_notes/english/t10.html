Narration (1): Statements, the Four Changes

Error Pro bank, 229 questions · most tested: modals (will → would, can → could), past → past perfect, present perfect → past perfect, "said to → told", truths and conditionals that never change
How to read this rule book Direct speech = the speaker's exact words inside quotes: He said, "I am tired." Indirect (reported) speech = you report the idea in your own sentence: He said that he was tired. The part outside the quotes (He said) is the reporting verb; the part inside is the reported speech.
Every rule has one plain line, one fresh example, and a table built from the bank (the Q column is the question number). In the "Given" column, highlighted = the word that must change. In the answer column, bold = the changed form. Some questions go direct → indirect, some go backwards (indirect → direct); the same rule works both ways, just reverse it. Q ×8 = how many bank questions test the rule. CGL+ = standard SSC point not in this bank.

Rule 1 · The reporting verb: said → said, said to → told; present or future → no tense change Q ×10

Rule: "said" (no listener) stays "said … that"; "said to + person" becomes "told + person … that". Never "told that" without a person, never "said me". If the reporting verb is in the present or future (says, will say), the tense inside does not change; only the pronouns do.

✗ He told that he was busy. → ✓ He said that he was busy. · ✓ He told me that he was busy.

Rule 14 · Orders and requests → ordered / requested / advised + (not) to + V1 Q ×26

Rule: an imperative (a sentence that starts with a verb: Stay, Listen, Please pass, Don't worry) becomes "reporting verb + person + to + V1".
