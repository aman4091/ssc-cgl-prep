Indus Valley Civilisation — the first planned cities

  • Planned cities: a raised citadel (west) and a lower town (east).
  • Grid pattern: roads cut each other at about right angles — the PYQ word is a rectangular layout. PYQ
  • Covered drains: each house was linked to a covered street drain PYQ. The system was advanced and planned for the whole town, not only for royals. PYQ
  • Standardised bricks (burnt bricks in a fixed size ratio of about 1:2:4). CGL+
  • What they did not have: iron tools PYQ, and big monumental temples, palaces or pyramids like Egypt or Mesopotamia — the PYQ answer for "Harappan architecture notably lacks" is monumental religious structures like temples or pyramids. PYQ
  • Seals were made of steatite and carry animal motifs and an undeciphered script. PYQ

Mauryan architecture and art

Main features: Ashokan pillars, highly polished sandstone (the "Mauryan polish"), rock-cut caves and stupas. Great advances came in Ashoka's reign. PYQ

  • Ashokan pillars are usually monolithic (one stone) and carved from sandstone (Chunar) PYQ.
  • Animal capitals: lion, bull, elephant, horse. An eagle is not found on them. PYQ
  • Persian (Achaemenid) influence shows in their bold and massive realism (and the bell-shaped capital). PYQ Indian difference: Mauryan shafts are one piece; Persian ones were in segments.
  • Pillar sites: Lauriya Nandangarh – Bihar (lion capital) PYQ; Rampurva (bull), Sankisa (elephant). CGL+
  • Dhauli (Odisha): front of an elephant carved out of the rock above Ashoka's edicts. PYQ
  • Life-size Yakshi holding a chauri (Didarganj Yakshi, Patna) = Mauryan period. PYQ
Lion Capital of SarnathMade in Ashoka's reign. Four lions sitting back to back on a round abacus (the abacus below shows an elephant, a horse, a bull and a lion, with wheels between them). Adopted as the State Emblem of India (26 January 1950 CGL+). Motto below it: Satyameva Jayate, taken from the Mundaka Upanishad. CGL+
Trick: State Emblem = Sarnath lion capital. "S-S": Sarnath → Satyameva Jayate. Pillar animals "L-B-E-H" = Lion, Bull, Elephant, Horse — Eagle never (Mauryan pillar pe "E" sirf Elephant).

Which Harappan site had a dockyard, and which had ploughed-field marks?

Lothal (Gujarat) had the dockyard; Kalibangan (Rajasthan) had criss-cross plough marks.

Harmika: what is it, and are the relics kept there?

The square railing on top of the stupa dome, a symbolic abode of the gods. Relics are kept inside the anda (dome), not in the harmika.

Chaitya vs vihara?

Chaitya = prayer/congregational hall. Vihara = monastery where monks live.

Which animal is NOT found on Mauryan pillar capitals: lion, bull, elephant or eagle?

Eagle.

The State Emblem of India comes from which monument?

The Lion Capital of Sarnath (Ashoka's reign).

What "rock-cut" means

Instead of building with bricks, the craftsmen carved a hill from the outside in — halls, pillars and even whole temples come out of one rock. The PDF's list of big examples: Ajanta, Ellora, Elephanta, Karla, Bhaja, Udayagiri, Barabar.

Ajanta — the painted caves

  • Mainly famous for Buddhist paintings: Jataka stories, the Buddha and Bodhisattvas, plus court and social life.
  • Most famous painting: Padmapani Bodhisattva (Cave 1). PYQ "Descent of the Ganges" is not Ajanta — it is a Pallava rock relief at Mahabalipuram.
  • Ajanta is the only surviving site with paintings from both the 1st c. BCE and the 5th c. CE — true. The reason "later phase had only vihara caves" is false, since later chaitya halls (Caves 19, 26) exist. Answer: A true, R false. PYQ
  • Rediscovered in 1819 by a British officer (John Smith) on a tiger hunt; UNESCO 1983. CGL+

Ellora and the Kailasa temple

  • Ellora has 34 caves of three faiths: Buddhist (1–12), Hindu (13–29), Jain (30–34). CGL+
  • Kailasa (Kailasanatha) temple: at Ellora, dedicated to Shiva, carved from one rock top to bottom. Built by the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I PYQ in the 8th century, in Maharashtra PYQ.

Elephanta and Barabar

  • Elephanta: island caves near Mumbai, mainly Shaivism. Star sculpture: the three-headed Trimurti / Maheshmurti of Shiva.
  • Barabar: Bihar, Mauryan period, linked with Ashoka and Dasharatha, gifted to the Ajivika sect; walls polished like glass.
⚠ Small correctionThe PDF puts Ashoka and Dasharatha on the Barabar caves. Strictly, the Barabar hill caves carry Ashoka's gifts; his grandson Dasharatha's caves are on the next hill, Nagarjuni. Exams usually club them as "Barabar–Nagarjuni caves", so "Barabar → Mauryan → Ajivikas" is safe.
Trick (PDF chains, re-worded): Kailasa → Ellora → Krishna I → Rashtrakuta ("K-E-K-R"). Barabar → Mauryan → rock-cut → Ajivikas ("B-M-A"). Ajanta = Art (paintings), Ellora = Every religion, Elephanta = Ek Shiva (Trimurti).

Where it began: Gupta temples

  • Free-standing (structural) temples in stone and brick began under the Gupta dynasty. PYQ Before that, shrines were of wood or cut into rock.
  • Bhitargaon temple — a brick temple with a tall shikhara, in Kanpur district, UP. PYQ
  • Dashavatara temple, Deogarh (Lalitpur, UP) — the earliest Panchayatana temple CGL+. A Panchayatana layout has five shrines: one main shrine in the centre and four at the corners. PYQ
  • Sanchi Temple 17 (flat roof) is among the earliest Gupta temples. CGL+

Akbar — Fatehpur Sikri and Buland Darwaza

  • Fatehpur Sikri (near Agra), built by Akbar. Main buildings: Buland Darwaza, Jama Masjid, Panch Mahal, Diwan-i-Khas, Tomb of Salim Chishti.
  • Buland Darwaza: built by Akbar to mark his Gujarat victory (1573; gate finished about 1575–76 CGL+).
  • Ibadat Khana (House of Worship), Fatehpur Sikri, 1575: for debates among scholars of different faiths PYQ; it best stands for his policy of Sulh-i-Kul (peace with all). PYQ
  • Agra Fort: a UNESCO site (1983), so "it is NOT UNESCO" is false; it was the main residence of the Mughal emperors — answer Only II. PYQ
  • Akbar's Tomb, Sikandra: a five-storey pyramid-like building with no main dome; it deviates from the square hasht-bihisht plan. PYQ
⚠ Small correctionThe PDF lists the "Tomb of Akbar → Sikandra" among Akbar's buildings. Akbar planned and began it, but it was completed by Jahangir (about 1613). If asked "who built Akbar's tomb", the answer is Jahangir.

Jahangir and Nur Jahan — Itimad-ud-Daulah

Indian painting: Bhimbetka to Bani Thani to Warli

Asked in Phase 13, Steno 2025, Steno 2026, Phase-XIV 2026 · most asked: Bhimbetka colours, Padmapani (Ajanta), Pandya murals, Humayun and Persian art, Amrita Sher-Gil

Six families of Indian painting

The PDF's six groups: 1 Prehistoric · 2 Buddhist · 3 Mughal · 4 Rajput/Rajasthani · 5 Pahari · 6 Folk & Tribal. The first two are wall paintings (murals); Mughal, Rajasthani and Pahari are mostly miniatures (small paintings on paper).

Prehistoric — Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh)

  • Bhimbetka rock shelters, near Bhopal (Raisen district), MP — famous for prehistoric rock paintings; UNESCO 2003. CGL+
  • Themes: hunting, animals, human activities, community life (dancing).
  • Colours were mineral: black, white and red ochre (plus some green and yellow). PYQ
  • Found by V.S. Wakankar (1957). CGL+
Trick: Bhimbetka = Bhut purana (very old) → prehistoric. Colours "Kala–Safed–Geru" (black, white, red ochre).

Buddhist — Ajanta (and South Indian murals)

  • Ajanta, Maharashtra; mainly Buddhist: Jataka stories, Buddha, Bodhisattvas, court and social life.
  • Most famous: Padmapani Bodhisattva (Cave 1). PYQ Technique: fresco-secco style on dry plaster (tempera). CGL+
  • Pandya-period murals in the Sittanavasal and Tirumalaipuram caves (Tamil Nadu) are key survivors — true; but they are on ceilings and pillars inside, not "outer walls with war scenes" — answer A true, R false. PYQ
  • Bagh caves (MP) also have Gupta-age murals. CGL+

Mughal painting

Features: miniatures, court scenes, portraits, battles, hunting, manuscript illustrations.

Sculpture schools: Gandhara, Mathura, Amaravati

Before the schools: the Buddha without a body

Early Buddhist art (Sanchi, Bharhut, about 2nd–1st c. BCE) never showed the Buddha as a man. This is the aniconic tradition of early Buddhism. PYQ He was shown by symbols:

SymbolStands for
Horse (riderless)Leaving home (renunciation)
Bodhi treeEnlightenment
Wheel (dharmachakra)First sermon at Sarnath
StupaMahaparinirvana (death)
Footprints / empty seatHis presence

The Buddha in human form first appeared about the 1st c. CE, under the Kushanas, through the Gandhara and Mathura schools. PYQ

  • Order of Buddhist sculpture by style: Sanchi Stupa-1 → Bharhut → Mathura (c-b-a). PYQ

Gandhara — the facts asked

  • Art born from a fusion of Greek and Indian styles = Gandhara. PYQ
  • Key difference: Mathura indigenous, Gandhara Hellenistic. PYQ
  • A–R: Gandhara Buddhas show Hellenistic features; the head has curly/wavy hair — both true, R explains A. PYQ
  • Not only stone: the gold Bimaran reliquary (figures in an arcade) and the carved Begram ivories — both correct. PYQ

Mathura — the facts asked

  • Best described by indigenous features with yaksha influence. PYQ
  • Made images for both Indian and non-Indian (Kushana) patrons — true; it did not ignore nature (yakshis with trees are famous) — answer only I correct. PYQ
  • Chaturmukha (four-faced) Shiva Linga = Mathura school. PYQ
  • Ushnisha on the Buddha's head = a bump (sign of wisdom), not a crown. PYQ
  • The Katra (Katra Keshav Dev) mound, Mathura — remains of temples and monasteries. PYQ
  • Kushana-period Mathura works: Govind Nagar Yaksha, standing Buddha, Katra finds. Not Mathura: the Bala Buddha of Sultanganj (a Gupta-age copper Buddha from Bihar). PYQ
  • The headless statue of Kanishka was found at Mat, near Mathura. CGL+

After the three schools

  • Sarnath school (Gupta): smooth, transparent robes, calm Buddha; the Sarnath "Dharmachakra-pravartana" Buddha. CGL+
  • Nalanda school (Pala, Bihar): three-dimensional, richly ornamented images; grew out of Sarnath, local Bihar and central Indian styles — both true, R explains A. PYQ
  • Chola bronzes: Nataraja made by the lost-wax (cire perdue) method. CGL+
⚠ Material note: AmaravatiThe PDF calls Amaravati's stone "white limestone". That is right; many books and SSC options call it "white marble" (it is a greenish-white Palnad limestone). Treat both as the same answer.
