BNS – BNSS – BSA: the three new criminal laws

Asked again and again in Phase 13, Steno 2025, CGL 2025, CHSL 2025, Steno 2026 and Phase-XIV 2026 · most asked: which law replaced which, Zero FIR, community service, mob lynching, electronic evidence

The big picture in one table

In 2023 Parliament threw out the three British-era criminal laws and put three Indian ones in their place. The aim, as the government put it: move from punishment (dand) to justice (nyay), with citizen-friendly, time-bound, digital processes. PYQ ×2 (answer: focus on justice delivery and citizen rights)

  • They came into force (general commencement) on 1 July 2024. One exception: BNS Section 106(2) was kept out of the 1 July 2024 start.
  • Bills passed by Lok Sabha on 20 Dec and Rajya Sabha on 21 Dec 2023; piloted by Home Minister Amit Shah. CGL+

Trick: Act numbers go in the order of a case: 45 → 46 → 47 = N → N S → S. Nyaya (what is the crime?) → Nagarik Suraksha (what is the procedure after the crime?) → Sakshya (what evidence proves the crime?). "Pehle crime, phir process, phir proof."

BNS, 2023 — what is a crime and what is the punishment

BNS replaced the IPC. It is the substantive criminal law: it lists offences and their punishments. The IPC itself was drafted by the first Law Commission under Lord Macaulay, set up under the Charter Act 1833 PYQ; IPC came into force on 1 Jan 1862. CGL+

Six big features of BNS

  • Community service is now a punishment — listed in Section 4 (the list of punishments). PYQ ×4 It is meant for petty, first-time offences (e.g. small theft under ₹5,000 with property returned, defamation, drunken misconduct in public) and is reformative: it cuts jail time and helps the offender rejoin society. In the A–R question both statements were true and R explained A.
  • A separate chapter for offences against women and children — Chapter V. PYQ
  • Organised crime is now a clear offence in the general criminal law.
  • Terrorist act is now an offence in BNS (earlier only in special laws like UAPA). PYQ
  • Mob lynching: murder by a group of 5 or more persons on grounds such as race, caste, community, sex, place of birth, language or personal belief. PYQ ×4
  • A new framework for acts against the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India (in place of "sedition").

  • Terrorism vs organised crime: terrorism aims to threaten national security; organised crime aims at unlawful gain. PYQ
  • "New offence under BNS?" — options Sedition / Criminal intimidation / Adultery / Terrorist act. Sedition and adultery are gone; criminal intimidation is old. PYQ
  • Sexual offences: the PYQ answer for "emphasised for the first time" was gender neutrality — some offences (for example those against children, like procuring a minor) now cover any gender. Marital rape is still not an offence. PYQ

  • Identity-theft type cyber fraud (stealing someone's details to create fake identities) is punishable — under BNS as cheating by personation, and under the IT Act. PYQ

BNSS, 2023 — the procedure after a crime

BNSS replaced the CrPC (Act No. 46 of 2023, in force 1 July 2024). It answers how: FIR, arrest, investigation, bail, trial. Its big theme is technology + time limits. PYQ ×2 (A–R on e-filing and virtual trials: both true, R explains A)

#FeatureIn plain words
1Zero FIRFile an FIR at any police station, whatever the jurisdiction; it is then sent to the right station. PYQ ×3 (§173) CGL+
2e-FIR / electronic reportingInformation about a crime can be given by electronic communication. E-FIRs permitted PYQ ×2. The informant must sign within 3 days. CGL+

6Investigation timelineOffences against women and children: investigation to finish within 2 months of recording the information.

  • "Electronic communication" in BNSS = any information sent using electronic devices like phones or computers. PYQ
  • FIR access for citizens through CCTNS (Crime and Criminal Tracking Network & Systems). PYQ Every police station keeps a Daily Case Register and a register of arrested persons. PYQ
  • Cybercrimes are investigated by cybercrime investigation cells set up by the government. PYQ
  • Cognizable offence (§2(1)(g)): police can arrest without warrant and investigate without a magistrate's order (murder, rape, theft). PYQ If police investigate a non-cognizable offence and report it, the report is deemed a complaint, the officer the complainant. PYQ
  • Arrested person must be taken before a magistrate within 24 hours (same as Art 22(2)). PYQ
  • Police custody: 15 days in total, can be taken in parts (§187). PYQ If the charge-sheet is not filed within 60 days (offences under 10 years) or 90 days (10 years+), the accused gets default bail — granted by the Magistrate. PYQ ×2
  • Witness protection: every State must notify a Witness Protection Scheme (§398) PYQ; witnesses can depose by video conferencing / with identity hidden. PYQ
  • Rape and related offences (BNS 64–71) are to be tried, as far as practicable, by a court presided over by a woman (BNSS §21). PYQ
  • One trial, several offences: court may order sentences to run concurrently or consecutively (§25(1)). PYQ
  • "Summary trials limited to only serious offences" is false — summary trials are for petty cases. PYQ
Time-limit numbers in one line24 hours (magistrate) · 3 days (sign e-FIR) · 14 days (documents) · 15 days (police custody) · 30 → 45 days (judgment) · 60 days (charges / default bail) · 2 months (women–child investigation) · 90 days (victim update).

BSA, 2023 — proving the crime

BSA replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (Act No. 47 of 2023, in force 1 July 2024). It modernises evidence law PYQ ×2 and aligns it with forensic science and digital technology PYQ. Its main reform: digitisation of evidence — digital and electronic evidence is fully recognised. PYQ ×5 From July 2024 it is the framework for electronic evidence in courts PYQ, working alongside the IT Act, 2000. PYQ

  • Electronic/digital records count as documents and can be primary evidence, with the same legal effect as paper. PYQ ×4 (A–R: "BSA treats e-records as primary" — both true, R explains A)
  • Examples the law names: e-mails, mobile/smartphone messages, computer files, digital documents, CCTV recordings, server logs, smartphone and location data. PYQ

  • Old law fact asked in Steno 2026: under the Indian Evidence Act, Section 25 barred confessions to police and Section 26 barred confessions in police custody (unless before a magistrate). PYQ
  • Related Act: the Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022 replaced the Identification of Prisoners Act, 1920 and allows collection of biometric and biological data. PYQ

⚠ Answer-key noteOne CGL 2025 question (21 Sep, Shift 2) asked "What digital reform was included under Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for courts?" and the key says virtual court hearings. Virtual/electronic hearings are really in BNSS (Section 530), not BNS — BNS only lists offences and punishments. In the exam, pick "virtual hearings" if it is the only digital option, but remember the correct home is BNSS.

Check yourself

BNS, BNSS, BSA — which Act numbers, and which old laws did they replace?

BNS = Act 45, replaced IPC 1860. BNSS = Act 46, replaced CrPC 1973. BSA = Act 47, replaced Indian Evidence Act 1872.

How many sections in BNS and in BNSS?

BNS 358 (IPC had 511). BNSS 531 (CrPC had 484).

Which section of BNS has community service, and for what kind of offender?

Section 4 (punishments). For petty, first-time offences — reformative.

Mob lynching: minimum group size and maximum punishment?

5 or more persons; death or life imprisonment (BNS 103(2)).

Sedition has been replaced by which BNS section?

Section 152 — acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.

An undertrial first-time offender can be released after serving what part of the maximum term?

One-third (BNSS Section 479).

Under BSA, which section requires a certificate for electronic records?

Section 63 (old Evidence Act 65B).
