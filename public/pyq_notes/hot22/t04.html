Art & architecture: the 8 pairs SSC loves to mix up

The PDF closes its art chapter with eight "A vs B" pairs · asked around them: Dravida vs Nagara features, Gandhara vs Mathura, Ajanta murals, Vesara

The eight pairs in one table

Each pair has one fact that separates the two. Learn that one fact and the option becomes easy.

PYQs asked around these pairs

  • The most distinctive Dravida feature vs Nagara: towering gopurams PYQ; also a water tank inside the temple enclosure PYQ. A Nagara temple usually has neither boundary wall nor gopuram.
  • Jagati in a Nagara temple = the raised base platform on which the temple stands. PYQ
  • Vesara = a mix of Nagara and Dravida (Chalukyas at Aihole, Pattadakal; later the Hoysalas). PYQ ×2
  • Mandapa = pillared hall. PYQ Panchayatana layout = five shrines (main shrine + four corner shrines). PYQ
  • The Buddha was first shown in human form by the Gandhara and Mathura schools (Kushana age, about 1st century CE). PYQ Before that, early stupas (Sanchi, Bharhut) used only symbols (tree, wheel, footprints), because early Buddhism was aniconic. PYQ
  • Gandhara used grey-blue schist; in its later phase it used mud, lime and stucco. PYQ
  • Mathura school: the ushnisha on the Buddha's head is a bump (cranial bump) PYQ; the Chaturmukha Shiva linga belongs to the Mathura school. PYQ
  • Ajanta's most famous painting: Padmapani Bodhisattva (Cave 1). PYQ Main phase of Ajanta was under the Vakatakas (Gupta-age contemporaries). PYQ
  • Do not mix Brihadeeswara with the Nataraja temple: Shiva as Nataraja = Chidambaram. PYQ
  • Kailasa temple (Ellora, Cave 16) was built by the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I. PYQ
  • Brihadeeswara is a UNESCO site under "Great Living Chola Temples" (with Gangaikondacholapuram and Airavatesvara, Darasuram). Konark Sun Temple is also called the "Black Pagoda". CGL+

Why folk dances exist

A folk dance usually grows out of local life: a community, a festival, farming and the seasons, marriage, religion and regional customs. So the exam clue is often a community ("snake charmers"), a festival ("Navratri") or an object ("pots", "sticks", "drum").

Swara and shruti

  • The 7 shuddha (pure) swaras: Sa – Re – Ga – Ma – Pa – Dha – Ni (Shadja, Rishabh, Gandhar, Madhyam, Pancham, Dhaivat, Nishad CGL+).
  • Vikrit (changed) swaras: komal (flat) forms of Re, Ga, Dha, Ni; tivra (sharp) form of Ma.
  • Achala (fixed) swaras: Sa and Pa — they never change.
  • Traditional theory: 7 swaras are spread over 22 shrutis (micro-tones).
  • So one octave has 12 notes: 7 shuddha + 4 komal + 1 tivra. CGL+
Trick: "SaPa sthir hai" (Sa and Pa stay put). "Ma ko tivra gussa aata hai" (only Ma gets sharp). Everyone else (Re Ga Dha Ni) is komal (soft).

Raga and tala

Raga = an organised melodic framework that creates a particular mood (bhava). Tala = a rhythmic cycle. (A PYQ swapped them as a trap: "raga = rhythm, tala = melody" is wrong.)
