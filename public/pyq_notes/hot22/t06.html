Pulses

Green Revolution — the PYQ points

  • Began in the mid-1960s, in the Third Five Year Plan period (1961–66). PYQ ×3 It used HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers and irrigation.
  • First state to benefit: Punjab (then Haryana, western UP). PYQ Largely ineffective in its first phase in Bihar. PYQ
  • Why regional disparity? HYV seed use was limited to well-irrigated regions. PYQ
  • Swaminathan (1925–2023) was given the Bharat Ratna in 2024 as architect of the Green Revolution; he also won the first World Food Prize (1987). PYQ
  • The First Five Year Plan (1951–56) gave top priority to agriculture and irrigation. PYQ ×3
  • Technology Mission on Oilseeds: run by the Ministry of Agriculture PYQ; NDDB's role was oilseed procurement and processing. PYQ

Eight soil types (ICAR classification)

ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) groups Indian soils into 8 types. CGL+ Alluvial is the most widespread (about 40% of area); black is second. CGL+

1. Alluvial soil

  • Laid down by rivers. Found in the Northern Plains, river valleys and coastal deltas — Punjab, Haryana, UP, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam, parts of Rajasthan and Gujarat.
  • Rich in potash, poor in phosphorus. CGL+ Texture runs from sandy loam to clay; red and yellow soils are sandy loam and less fertile on uplands. PYQ
  • Crops: rice, wheat, sugarcane, maize, pulses, jute. Ganga basin, India's biggest agricultural basin in the north, owes its fertility to alluvium. PYQ
KhadarBhangar
AgeNew alluviumOld alluvium
WhereLow flood plains, renewed by floods every yearHigher terraces above flood level — the largest part of the northern plain PYQ
FertilityMore fertileLess fertile
MarkerFine siltKankar (lime nodules)
Trick: Khadar = Khush (fresh, fertile, new). Bhangar = Bhooda (old) + Kankar.
  • North of the plains: Bhabar = a narrow belt of pebbles and gravel at the foot of the Shiwaliks (streams vanish into it) PYQ; then Terai = wet, marshy belt where the streams come back up. CGL+

2. Black soil (Regur)

  • Made from basalt lava of the Deccan Trap — the Deccan Trap rocks are igneous, and the region is covered by black soil. PYQ ×2
  • States: Maharashtra (largest), MP, Gujarat, Telangana, Karnataka, AP, Tamil Nadu. Found extensively in Maharashtra and ideal for cotton → black soil. PYQ
  • High clay, holds moisture for long; deep cracks in the dry season let air in — so it is "self-ploughing". Sticky when wet.
  • Rich in calcium (lime), magnesium, iron (also potash, alumina); poor in nitrogen, phosphorus, humus.
  • PYQ (which features fit black soil?): "high moisture retention with deep cracks" ✔, "rich in lime, iron, magnesia, alumina but poor in phosphorus" ✔, "ideal for tea and apples in cold climate" ✘ (that is mountain soil) → only 1 and 3. PYQ
  • Main crop cotton (so it is also called "black cotton soil"); also soybean, groundnut, wheat, sugarcane, tobacco.

3. Red & yellow soil

  • Red colour from iron (iron oxide) spread through old crystalline rocks; looks yellow when hydrated. CGL+
  • Forms on igneous (granite, gneiss) rocks in low-rain areas of the east and south Deccan — "Red soil — igneous origin" is the correct pair. PYQ
  • Low humus, low nitrogen, often acidic. States: TN, Karnataka, AP, Telangana, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand.

4. Laterite soil

  • Forms under high rain + high temperature, which cause intense leaching (rain washes nutrients down). Name from Latin later = brick. CGL+
  • Rich in iron and aluminium; poor in nitrogen, potash, organic matter; usually acidic.
  • Regions: Western Ghats, Karnataka, Kerala, TN, Odisha, Maharashtra, North-East.
  • With manure it suits tea, coffee, rubber and cashew. Correct pair: Laterite soil – cashew nuts. PYQ
Trick: Laterite = "Lagataar baarish" (non-stop rain) → leaching → only iron + aluminium left (like bricks).

Soil erosion and conservation

Soil erosion = removal of the topsoil by water, wind or human activity. The gradual wearing away of Earth's surface by wind, water and ice is called erosion. PYQ

  • Major human cause: deforestation PYQ ×2; also overgrazing, faulty farming, mining. Fertile land turning into desert = desertification. PYQ
  • Main cause of soil pollution: chemical fertilisers and pesticides. PYQ
Erosion typeWhat happens
SheetA thin, even layer of topsoil is washed off a wide area
GullyRunning water cuts deep channels (gullies) → land becomes badlands / ravines
WindDry, loose soil blown away in arid regions
  • Gully erosion classic = Chambal ravines, spread over Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. PYQ A–R: Chambal is known for badland ravines because it flows through deeply eroded terrain → both true, R explains A. PYQ
Trick (from the PDF, reworded): "Pahad pe seedhi (terrace), dhalaan pe rekha (contour), hawa ke aage pedon ki deewar (shelter belt)." Gully erosion → think Chambal.

  • Rural–urban trend 1901–2011: rural numbers kept rising, the rural share kept falling, and urban population crossed 100 million in 1981 → all three statements true. PYQ

Demographic transition theory

  • Stage II: death rate declines, birth rate stays high. PYQ Stage III: both birth and death rates decline PYQ; fertility approaches replacement level. PYQ
  • Population stabilises when birth and death rates are nearly equal. PYQ Population explosion = deaths fall fast while births stay high; social behaviour does not change instantly (A true, R false). PYQ
  • The theory does not take migration into account. A CGL 2025 key chose 1, 2 and 3 as the correct statements. PYQ
⚠ Answer-key noteIn that CGL question, statement 1 said high birth and death rates in the first two stages give high growth. Strictly, Stage 1 growth is slow (both rates high cancel out); fast growth comes in Stage 2. The key still marked 1, 2, 3 — remember the key, but learn the correct idea.

Migration

  • Factors come in 2 sets: push (drive people out — civil unrest, no jobs) and pull (draw people in — jobs, schools). PYQ ×3 "Better job opportunities" is a pull factor, not a push factor. PYQ
  • Leaving one's country to settle abroad = emigration. PYQ
  • Rural→urban migration is a major source of urban growth, pulled by jobs and education PYQ; pushed by lack of farm employment. PYQ (Overall, Census 2011 shows marriage as the top reason, mainly for women. CGL+)
  • Problem for destination cities: exploitation of unskilled migrants and growth of informal work. PYQ Shift of workers from farming to industry/services = occupational diversification. PYQ
  • North-Eastern states show high female work participation — both statements correct (tribal societies share work more equally). PYQ

Policy and projections (one-liners)

  • MoSPI 2024 report: by 2036 sex ratio 952, women 48.8% of population. PYQ ×2 Elderly to reach 193.4 million by 2031 (Technical Group, 2020). PYQ Economic Survey 2023-24: over 40% urban by 2030. PYQ

Minerals: types, the four mineral belts and metallic ores

Asked in Phase 13, Steno 2025, CGL 2025, CHSL 2025, Steno 2026 and Phase-XIV 2026 · most asked: iron ore mines and belts, which belt has what, bauxite and manganese leaders, ferrous vs non-ferrous

Mineral basics

  • Mineral = a natural, inorganic substance with a definite chemical composition and physical properties.
  • Ore = a mineral or rock from which a metal can be taken out profitably. Every ore contains minerals, but not every mineral is an ore.
  • India's minerals are unevenly spread. Most metallic minerals lie in the old crystalline rocks of the Peninsular Plateau; the Northern alluvial plains have very few economic minerals.
  • NCERT fact: most major minerals lie east of a line joining Mangaluru and Kanpur.
  • Most abundant mineral group in Earth's crust: feldspar. PYQ
  • India is rich in iron ore PYQ and in non-metallic minerals vital for major industries PYQ, but non-ferrous metals — copper, zinc, lead — are scarce. PYQ
  • Example of a non-ferrous metallic mineral: bauxite (not manganese, chromite or iron ore). PYQ
  • Non-metallic minerals mostly used in local markets: limestone, dolomite, phosphate. PYQ
  • Natural gas cannot be replenished quickly — it is non-renewable; its main component is methane. PYQ ×2

Atomic minerals

  • Monazite = a sand mineral that contains thorium and rare-earth elements. Found in the Kerala coastal sands (Chavara) CGL+, also Odisha and TN coasts. India has some of the world's largest thorium reserves. CGL+
