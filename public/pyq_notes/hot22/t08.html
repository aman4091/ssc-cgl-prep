GS Hot 22 · Modern History · British Acts (Part 1)

Company-era Acts: 1773 to 1853

Most asked in CGL 2025, CHSL 2025, Steno 2026 and Phase-XIV 2026: Pitt's India Act (asked 8+ times), Regulating Act, Charter Act 1813 and 1833, Act of Settlement 1781

The whole story in one line

After Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764) a trading company was ruling Bengal. The British Parliament slowly pulled the reins: first control (1773), then dual control (1784), then it took away the Company's trade (1813, 1833), and finally its rule (1858). Who built this structure of British administration in India? The British Parliament, through these Acts. PYQ

Trick: "R-A-P-A, then Charter every 20 years." Regulating 1773 → Amending 1781 → Pitt's 1784 → Act of 1786 → Charter 1793 → 1813 → 1833 → 1853. Charter Acts come every 20 years (93, 13, 33, 53) — the Company's licence was renewed for 20 years each time, except in 1853.

1. Regulating Act, 1773 — Parliament steps in

  • The first time the British Parliament took direct control over the East India Company's affairs. It was passed to control the administration of the Company. PYQ It also recognised, for the first time, the Company's political and administrative functions. CGL+
  • It changed two areas: the Company's constitution at home (in Britain) and its government in India (territorial). PYQ
  • The Governor of Bengal was raised to Governor-General of Bengal, with an executive council of 4 members. PYQ (Phase-XIV trap: "reduced to Governor of Fort William" is false. PYQ)
  • Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal.
  • The Governors of Bombay and Madras were made subordinate to the Governor-General of Bengal in some matters (mainly war and peace).
  • A Supreme Court at Calcutta was set up in 1774: one Chief Justice + three other judges. PYQ First Chief Justice: Sir Elijah Impey. PYQ
  • Court of Directors (the Company's governing body in London): term fixed at 4 years; one-fourth of the directors retired every year.
  • Company servants were barred from private trade and from taking gifts or bribes. CGL+
Before the Act: Hastings' Judicial Plan, 1772As Governor of Bengal, Warren Hastings set up district civil (Diwani) and criminal (Faujdari) courts. Cases were decided under Hindu law for Hindus and Muslim law for Muslims — a dual system respecting personal laws. PYQ

2. Amending Act (Act of Settlement), 1781

  • Why was it needed? The Regulating Act left the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court vague — the Court and the Governor-General-in-Council kept clashing. PYQ ×2
  • It defined the powers of the Supreme Court and of the Governor-General-in-Council.
  • Revenue matters and official acts of government servants were kept outside the Supreme Court's jurisdiction.
  • The Court was told to respect the personal laws of Indians. CGL+
⚠ Name checkThe PDF says the 1781 Act is "also called the Declaratory Act". Standard books call the 1781 Act the Act of Settlement (or Amending Act). The name Declaratory Act belongs to the 1788 Act, which made clear that the Board of Control could direct the Company. If an option says "Act of Settlement 1781", that is the one.

3. Pitt's India Act, 1784 — dual control (SSC favourite)

The most-asked Act of this period. It split the Company's work into two: politics went under the British Government, business stayed with the Company. It separated the commercial and political functions of the Company. PYQ The principle it set up: dual control between the Company and the British Government. PYQ ×2

BodyWhoLooked after
Board of Control (new)British Government — a six-member body PYQPolitical affairs: it was to control civil and military affairs and revenue PYQ
Court of Directors (old)CompanyCommercial affairs (trade)
  • For the first time the Company's territories were called "British possessions in India"; they now came under the British Crown's supervision. In the long run, it placed the Company under the British Parliament. PYQ
  • The Court of Directors became answerable to the British Government in civil and military matters. PYQ
  • The Governor-General and council were forbidden to declare war or sign treaties without the approval of the Directors or the Secret Committee. PYQ ×2
  • It made the Governor-General supreme over the Presidencies, unifying British administration. PYQ Madras and Bombay were made subordinate to Bengal in war, diplomacy and revenue — so the Phase-XIV statement "only in matters of revenue" is false. PYQ
  • Passed when William Pitt the Younger was British Prime Minister; Warren Hastings was the Governor-General. CGL+

4. Act of 1786

  • The Governor-General got the power to override his council in special cases.
  • He could also hold the post of Commander-in-Chief.
  • Passed so that Lord Cornwallis would accept the job — he asked for both powers.

5. Charter Act, 1793

  • Company's trade monopoly extended for another 20 years.
  • Company's political functions continued.
  • Salaries of the Board of Control's members and staff were to be paid out of Indian revenues.
  • The override power of the Governor-General (from 1786) was extended to the Governors of Bombay and Madras over their councils. CGL+

6. Charter Act, 1813 (very important)

  • Ended the Company's trade monopoly in India PYQ ×2 — except the trade in tea and trade with China. PYQ So, India was open to all British traders.
  • Allowed Christian missionaries to come to India — the most politically sensitive part of the Act. PYQ
  • Set aside ₹1 lakh a year for education of Indians — asked as the pair "Charter Act 1813 – education funds". PYQ
  • The Company kept control of its territories for another 20 years.
⚠ Wording fixThe PDF says "Company's territorial sovereignty continued". More exactly: the Company kept possession of its Indian territories, but the 1813 Act for the first time clearly stated the sovereignty of the British Crown over them.
Trick: 1813 = "Teen cheezein": trade khuli (except Tea + China), Teachers of religion (missionaries) aaye, Ten-thousand ×10 (₹1 lakh) for education.

7. Charter Act, 1833 (one of the MOST important)

  • The Governor-General of Bengal became the Governor-General of India. PYQ Lord William Bentinck = first Governor-General of India.
  • It was the final step towards centralisation: the Governor-General of India got all civil and military powers. CGL+
  • Bombay and Madras lost their law-making powers; all legislation was centralised.
  • Ended the Company's commercial activities completely (even tea and China). The Company became a purely administrative body.
  • Provided for the first Law Commission, headed by Lord Macaulay. PYQ (It drafted the IPC.) Macaulay was also the first Law Member of the council. CGL+
  • Tried to bring in open competition for civil service posts, but this part was not put into effect then.

8. Charter Act, 1853 (SSC favourite)

  • The last Charter Act.
  • Separated the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council.
  • Added 6 new legislative members to the council (the start of a small central legislative council, sometimes called a "mini-Parliament"). CGL+
  • Brought in open competition for the Civil Services, Indians included (Macaulay Committee, 1854). CGL+
  • The Company's rule was not renewed for a fixed 20-year term — it could be ended any time Parliament wished.
  • Created a separate Lieutenant-Governor for Bengal.

Crown-era Acts: 1858 to 1947

Most asked: 1858 Act (Viceroy, Council of India, bodies abolished), 1892 budget discussion, 1909 separate electorates, 1919 dyarchy + bicameral Centre, 1935 federal structure + Federal Court 1937

9. Government of India Act, 1858 (extremely important)

Passed after the Revolt of 1857. Also called the Act for the Good Government of India. CGL+

  • Rule of India passed from the East India Company to the British Crown. Crown rule began.
  • The Governor-General was also given the title of Viceroy (the Crown's direct representative). PYQ Lord Canning = first Viceroy.
  • Abolished the Board of Control and the Court of Directors — dual control ended. PYQ
  • Created a new post, the Secretary of State for India — a member of the British cabinet, answerable to Parliament. First one: Lord Stanley. CGL+
  • He was helped by a 15-member Council of India, whose role was to advise the Secretary of State. PYQ
  • Queen Victoria's Proclamation (read by Canning at Allahabad, 1 November 1858) promised to respect Indians' customary religious and social practices and ended further annexations. PYQ CGL+

10. Indian Councils Act, 1861

  • Start of Indian association with law-making: for the first time after 1857, Indians were brought into the legislative councils (as nominated non-official members).
  • In 1862 Lord Canning nominated three Indians: the Raja of Benares, the Maharaja of Patiala and Sir Dinkar Rao.
  • Restored law-making powers to Bombay and Madras (reversing 1833) — start of decentralisation.
  • Gave legal status to the portfolio system (started by Canning in 1859): each member of the council got a department.
  • The Viceroy could issue ordinances in an emergency (valid for 6 months). CGL+
⚠ Answer-key noteSteno 2026 (15 Sep, Shift 1) asked which Act first brought Indians into "the highest executive councils of the Viceroy and Governors". The key says Indian Councils Act 1861. But 1861 brought Indians only into the legislative council. Indians entered the Viceroy's executive council only under the 1909 Act (S. P. Sinha). Since 1909 was not among the options, 1861 was the "best available" answer — remember the real distinction.

11. Indian Councils Act, 1892

  • Increased the number of non-official members in the councils (not the official members). PYQ
  • Councils could discuss the budget and ask questions of the executive PYQ ×2 — but could not vote on the budget.
  • Brought in indirect election in a very limited form (members "recommended" by bodies like universities and municipal boards). Not direct election — that statement is false. PYQ
  • Passed under Viceroy Lord Lansdowne.

12. Indian Councils Act, 1909 — Morley–Minto Reforms

  • Named after John Morley (Secretary of State) and Lord Minto II (Viceroy).
  • Introduced separate electorates for Muslims — Muslim members to be elected only by Muslim voters. PYQ ×2 Nationalists attacked it because separate electorates based on religion were divisive. PYQ Lord Minto is called the Father of Communal Electorate. CGL+
  • Greatly enlarged the legislative councils, both central and provincial. PYQ Members could ask supplementary questions and move resolutions on the budget — so "did not permit" is false. PYQ
  • For the first time, an Indian joined the Viceroy's Executive Council: Satyendra Prasanna Sinha, as Law Member.
⚠ Spelling fixThe PDF writes "Satyendra Prasad Sinha" in one place. The correct name is Satyendra Prasanna Sinha (later Lord Sinha) — the PDF itself uses "Prasanna" in the Viceroys section.

13. Government of India Act, 1919 — Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms

  • Named after Edwin Montagu (Secretary of State) and Lord Chelmsford (Viceroy). Based on Montagu's August Declaration of 1917 (aim: "responsible government"). CGL+
  • Brought in dyarchy in the provinces PYQ ×2 — provincial subjects were split into Reserved and Transferred. It is seen as the Act that first gave Indians limited self-government. PYQ
  • Made the Centre bicameral — two houses PYQ: the Council of State (upper) and the Central Legislative Assembly (lower). PYQ Majority of members were directly elected.
  • Extended communal representation to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans.
  • Direct elections on a wider scale; franchise widened but still small (based on property, tax, education).
  • Provided for a Public Service Commission — the Central PSC was set up in 1926.
  • Provincial budgets were separated from the central budget. CGL+ A High Commissioner for India in London was created. CGL+
Trick: Reserved = "Paisa aur Police" (finance, revenue, law and order) — British ke paas. Transferred = "Padhai, Dawai, Gaon" (education, health, local bodies) — Indian ministers ke paas.

14. Government of India Act, 1935 (MOST important for SSC)

  • The longest and most detailed Act passed by the British for India (321 sections, 10 schedules). CGL+
  • Aim: a federal structure with provincial autonomy. PYQ ×3 This feature was later adopted in our Constitution. PYQ
  • Proposed an All-India Federation of provinces and princely states — never came into being (the princes did not join).
  • Provincial autonomy: dyarchy in provinces ended; provinces got responsible governments (began 1 April 1937).
  • Dyarchy at the Centre was proposed — never implemented.
  • Powers divided into three lists: Federal, Provincial and Concurrent. Residuary powers went to the Viceroy. CGL+
  • Federal Court set up in 1937. PYQ
  • Provided the framework for the Reserve Bank of India; the RBI itself was set up under the separate RBI Act, 1934 and began work on 1 April 1935. CGL+
  • Extended the franchise (about 10% of people could vote). CGL+
  • Separated Burma from India, effective 1937. PYQ
  • It is the biggest source of our Constitution: federal scheme, Governor's office, Judiciary, Public Service Commissions, emergency provisions. CGL+

15. Indian Independence Act, 1947 — the final British Act

  • Created two independent Dominions: India and Pakistan (based on the Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947).
  • British rule ended on 15 August 1947. Royal assent: 18 July 1947. CGL+
  • British paramountcy over the princely states ended; they could join India or Pakistan, or stay independent.
  • Office of the Secretary of State for India abolished.
  • The Constituent Assemblies of both countries became sovereign and also worked as their legislatures.
  • Lord Mountbatten became the first Governor-General of independent India; C. Rajagopalachari the first and only Indian Governor-General.

Warren Hastings (1773–1785)

  • Office created by the Regulating Act; Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774), first Chief Justice Sir Elijah Impey.
  • First Anglo-Maratha War 1775–1782, ended by the Treaty of Salbai (1782). PYQ
  • Second Anglo-Mysore War 1780–1784 (against Haidar Ali, then Tipu) — ended by the Treaty of Mangalore, 1784. CGL+
  • Pitt's India Act (1784) came during his term.
  • His foreign policy is called the Ring-Fence policy: protect the Company's borders by keeping friendly buffer states (like Awadh) around them. PYQ
  • The Asiatic Society of Bengal was founded in 1784 by William Jones, with Hastings' support. CGL+ Back in England he faced impeachment (1788–95) and was acquitted. CGL+

Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793)

  • Permanent Settlement of Bengal in 1793 PYQ — the SSC favourite: Permanent Settlement → Cornwallis → 1793.
  • Its main aim: create a class of loyal zamindars and a fixed, sure revenue. PYQ Before 1793 zamindars only collected revenue; after it, the zamindars (not the peasants) became owners of the land. So "peasants became owners" is false. PYQ
  • Cornwallis Code (1793): separated revenue and judicial work. He is called the Father of the Civil Services in India. CGL+
  • Third Anglo-Mysore War 1790–92 PYQ — Tipu was fought by the Company, the Marathas and the Nizam together PYQ; ended by the Treaty of Seringapatam (1792), Tipu gave up half his kingdom.
Related: the three land-revenue systems Permanent Settlement / Zamindari (Bengal, 1793) — Cornwallis; revenue fixed forever.
Ryotwari — Thomas Munro and Alexander Read PYQ ×2; started in the Madras Presidency (1792, Baramahal), later Bombay — so "started in Bombay in 1729" is wrong. PYQ Each peasant (ryot) pays directly to the state. PYQ
Mahalwari — Holt Mackenzie (1822) PYQ; whole village (mahal) assessed together. Its flaw: revenue was revised upward from time to time, burdening the whole village. PYQ

Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)

  • Introduced (popularised) the Subsidiary Alliance. Conditions for the Indian ruler: disband his own army and keep a permanent British force, paid for by him PYQ; keep a British Resident at his court PYQ; no dealings with other powers without British consent. First to sign: Hyderabad (1798). CGL+ (The idea was first used by the French governor Dupleix. CGL+)
  • Policy pair: Wellesley — imperialist conquests. PYQ
  • Fourth Anglo-Mysore War, 1799: Tipu Sultan died at Seringapatam. Tipu had become ruler in 1782 PYQ; his capital was Seringapatam PYQ; he modernised his army with French help. PYQ
  • Awadh was forced in 1801 to hand over about half its territory to the Company. PYQ
  • Treaty of Bassein, 1802 (with Peshwa Baji Rao II) → led to the Second Anglo-Maratha War 1803–05. CGL+
  • Fort William College, Calcutta, 1800 — to train Company officials.

Lord Hastings (1813–1823)

  • Anglo-Nepal (Gurkha) War 1814–16, ended by the Treaty of Sugauli (1816).
  • Third Anglo-Maratha War 1817–18 — the Peshwa's power ended in 1818; the Peshwa post was abolished. CGL+
  • Started the policy of Paramountcy (the Company is the supreme power; Indian states are below it). PYQ

Check yourself

Who was the only Viceroy assassinated in office, and where?

Lord Mayo, at Port Blair (Andamans), 1872.

The Ilbert Bill (1883) was about what, and under which Viceroy?

Allowing Indian judges to try European offenders; Lord Ripon.

Vernacular Press Act: year, Viceroy, and who repealed it?

1878, Lord Lytton; repealed by Lord Ripon in 1882.

Capital moved from Calcutta to Delhi in which year and under which Viceroy?

1911 (announced at the Delhi Durbar by King George V), Lord Hardinge II.

Who was the last Governor-General of India?

C. Rajagopalachari (1948–1950), also the first and only Indian GG.
