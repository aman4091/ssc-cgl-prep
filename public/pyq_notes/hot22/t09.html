Heart and blood vessels

  • The human heart has 4 chambers: 2 atria (top, receiving) and 2 ventricles (bottom, pumping).
  • Right side → deoxygenated blood; left side → oxygenated blood. The separate chambers exist to stop oxygen-rich and CO₂-rich blood from mixing. PYQ
  • Used blood from the body enters the right atrium PYQ. Order: right atrium → right ventricle → (lungs) → left atrium → left ventricle PYQ.
  • Natural pacemaker = SA node (sino-atrial node). A normal heart beats about 72 times a minute. CGL+
  • Largest artery = aorta; largest vein = vena cava.
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart PYQ. Their walls are thick and elastic to bear high blood pressure PYQ. Veins bring blood back and have valves; capillaries are the thinnest vessels, where exchange happens. CGL+
  • Exception pair: the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood. CGL+
  • Normal BP = 120/80 mm Hg (systolic/diastolic) PYQ. Pressure while the heart relaxes = diastolic PYQ. Measured with a sphygmomanometer. CGL+
Trick: Artery = Away from heart. Right side = Return (body ka used blood), Left side = Leave (fresh blood bahar jaata hai).

Molecular facts that were asked

  • Pairing: A pairs with T by two hydrogen bonds PYQ; G pairs with C by three. CGL+
  • Chargaff's rule: A = T and G = C. If A = 20%, then T = 20%, so G + C = 60% and G = 30%. PYQ
  • Flow of genetic information (central dogma): DNA → RNA → protein PYQ. Transcription = DNA is copied into RNA PYQ; translation = RNA → protein. CGL+
  • The promoter provides the binding site for RNA polymerase. PYQ
  • A nucleosome = negatively charged DNA wrapped around a positively charged histone octamer. PYQ
  • Telomerase does telomere lengthening. PYQ
  • Hershey–Chase used radioactive phosphorus to label viral DNA, because phosphorus is in DNA but not in protein (sulphur was used for protein). It proved DNA is the genetic material. PYQ ×2 Phosphorus forms the backbone of nucleic acids. PYQ
  • Pleiotropy = one gene affects many traits. PYQ
  • Hugo de Vries said new species arise mainly from large mutations. PYQ
Trick: "A–T do, G–C teen" (A–T 2 bonds, G–C 3). RNA mein T ki jagah U — "RNA U-turn leta hai".

Mendel's three laws

LawWhat it says
DominanceIn a hybrid (heterozygous) pair, the dominant trait hides the recessive one.
SegregationThe two alleles separate when gametes form, so each gamete gets only one ("law of purity of gametes"). CGL+
Independent assortmentDifferent gene pairs sort into gametes independently — true only for genes that are not linked.

Convex mirror

  • For any object position the image is always virtual, erect and diminished.
  • It forms behind the mirror, between P and F.
  • Main use: rear-view / side-view mirror in vehicles, because it gives a wide field of view. Also used in street lights and shop security mirrors. CGL+

Refraction — rules

SituationWhat happens

SpeedChanges
WavelengthChanges
FrequencyDoes not change PYQ
  • Refractive index n = c / v (speed in vacuum ÷ speed in the medium). Higher n → slower light.
  • Typical values: water 1.33, glass about 1.5, diamond 2.42 (highest of common materials). CGL+
  • Snell's law: sin i / sin r = constant (= relative refractive index). CGL+

Trick: "Frequency is Fixed" — source decides frequency, medium decides speed. Dense medium = bheed → light slow, bends towards normal (sehma hua).

Total internal reflection (TIR)

Both conditions must hold: (1) light goes from a denser to a rarer medium, and (2) the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle. Denser → rarer, at more than the critical angle. PYQ

Dispersion, rainbow, blue sky and the electromagnetic spectrum

Asked in CGL 2025, Steno 2025–26 and Phase 13 · most asked: shortest wavelength = gamma, secondary rainbow order, UV absorbed by ozone, EM waves need no medium

Dispersion

  • Dispersion = white light splitting into its colours (first shown with a glass prism by Isaac Newton CGL+).
  • Order: VIBGYOR — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red.
  • Violet deviates the most, red the least. Reason: violet has the shortest wavelength, so glass slows it most (higher refractive index for violet). CGL+

Rainbow

  • A rainbow forms by refraction, dispersion and internal reflection of sunlight inside raindrops.
  • It is seen with the Sun behind you (opposite side of the sky). CGL+
  • Primary rainbow: red on the outside, violet inside. CGL+ Secondary rainbow (two internal reflections): colour order is reversed. PYQ

Scattering — blue sky, red sunset

ObservationReason
Sky looks blueAir molecules scatter shorter wavelengths (blue) much more strongly than longer ones (Rayleigh scattering, ∝ 1/λ⁴ CGL+)
Sun looks reddish at sunrise and sunsetLight crosses a much longer path of air; most blue is scattered away on the way, so the longer wavelengths (red, orange) reach the eye
Danger signals are red CGL+Red is scattered least, so it is seen from far, even in fog
Clouds look white CGL+Large water droplets scatter all colours almost equally
Sky looks black to astronauts CGL+No atmosphere up there, so nothing to scatter light
  • Raman effect (change in wavelength when light scatters off molecules) — C.V. Raman, 1928; Nobel Prize 1930; 28 February is National Science Day. CGL+
  • Wave behaviour also asked: interference is a direct result of the superposition principle PYQ; a wave bending through a narrow slit is diffraction. PYQ

Electromagnetic spectrum (SSC trap)

Increasing wavelength: Gamma < X-ray < UV < Visible < Infrared < Microwave < Radio. Frequency and energy go the other way.

  • Highest frequency (and shortest wavelength) = gamma rays. Shortest wavelength asked: gamma rays. PYQ
  • Lowest frequency (longest wavelength) = radio waves.
  • Visible light ≈ 400–700 nm (violet ≈ 400, red ≈ 700).
  • All EM waves are transverse and need no medium. Maxwell showed that a changing electric field makes a magnetic field and vice versa, so the wave sustains itself even in vacuum at c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s. PYQ
