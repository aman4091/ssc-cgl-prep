Homonyms I: One Spelling, Two Meanings

SSC CGL Sep–Oct 2025 (one per shift) + Stenographer Aug 2025 · task: "pick the sentence where the word has a DIFFERENT meaning" · most asked: noun ↔ verb switches, nature words, church words
How to read this chapter A homonym = same spelling, different meaning (often a different sound too). SSC CGL 2025 asked it in one fixed way: a sentence with a highlighted word, then four sentences. Three use the same meaning; one uses a different meaning — that one is the answer. In each table: the bold word was asked, the first meaning is the sense in the question, and the highlighted meaning is the sense in the correct option. Q = asked; Q ×2 = asked twice. CGL+ = a standard homonym of the same type that was not in this bank but is common in exams.
Trick (method): Teen options ek group, ek "odd man out". Har option mein word ko ek Hindi shabd se badlo — jo option alag Hindi shabd maange, wahi answer. Example: bark = chhaal / chhaal / chhaal / bhaunkna → answer.

Homonyms II: Meaning in Context and Homophones

SSC Stenographer Aug 2025 ("select the most appropriate homonym" = meaning in context) + older fill-in-the-blank homophone sets · most asked: law words, scent/sent/cent, flour/floor/flower

B. Homophones: words that sound alike but differ in spelling and meaning (rain / rein / reign). Fill the blank with the right spelling. Highlighted = the keyed answer; bold = asked word or wrong option.

How to read the idiom chapters (I–IV) The 350 idiom questions are grouped by picture (body, animal, food…) or by meaning family (trouble, quarrel, success, time).
