Idioms I · Courage, challenge, effort and risk

Most asked: bite the bullet (×3), face the music (×3), thin ice (×3), the gauntlet trio, throw in the towel, effort idioms (no stone unturned, strain every nerve)
How to read this chapterIdioms are grouped by meaning family, not by question order. Each table gives the idiom, its meaning, a Hinglish hint and the origin or opposite. Q = asked in the bank (Q ×2 = asked twice). Highlighted meaning = the keyed answer; when the idiom itself was the answer (fill-in / substitute questions), the idiom is highlighted. Rows with no tag = wrong options of those questions (distractors) — SSC reuses them as answers. CGL+ = standard idiom of the same family, not in this set.

Check yourself

Bite the bullet vs take the bull by the horns?

Both are brave. Bite the bullet = endure something painful; bull by the horns = attack the problem directly.

Throw down the gauntlet vs take up the gauntlet?

Throw down = issue a challenge; take up / pick up = accept it.

What does 'to show the white feather' mean?

To act like a coward.

Throw one's hat into the ring?

To enter a competition or election.

Cold feet vs itchy feet?

Cold feet = nervous, wanting to back out; itchy feet = wanting to travel or change.
