The Stone Age to the Bronze Age, in one line

  • Mesolithic hunters used barbed spears and arrows made by fitting microliths on wood or bone. PYQ Swords, catapults and cannons belong to much later ages.

The Indus Valley Civilization: the basics

  • It is the oldest known civilization of the subcontinent — older than the Vedic age, the Mauryas and the Guptas. PYQ
  • Other names: Harappan (after the first site found), Indus, Sindhu–Sarasvati. "Mesopotamian Civilization" is NOT one of its names — that is a separate civilization in Iraq (Tigris–Euphrates). PYQ
  • The Greeks called the region around the Indus "Indoi" / "Indike", from Persian "Hindu", from Sanskrit "Sindhu". PYQ "India" grew out of this.
  • Economy ran on barter — no coins have been found. PYQ Standard weights (in multiples of 16) kept trade fair. CGL+
  • Mesopotamian records call the Indus region Meluhha. CGL+
  • CGL+ Harappa was dug first in 1921 by Daya Ram Sahni; Sir John Marshall was ASI Director-General then. Mohenjodaro: R.D. Banerji, 1922.

Sites to place: state, river, famous find

  • Harappa stands on the Ravi (not the Indus). PYQ Mohenjodaro is on the Indus.
  • Chanhudaro = a craft town for bead-making (also shell and bangle work). PYQ Only big Harappan city with no citadel. CGL+
  • Kalibangan ("black bangles") shows a field with criss-cross plough marks — two crops in one field. PYQ
  • Rakhigarhi is in Haryana (Hisar). PYQ Daimabad is in Maharashtra (Ahmednagar) — the southernmost site. PYQ
  • Red carnelian for beads came mostly from Gujarat. PYQ
  • CGL+ Extreme sites: north Manda (J&K, Chenab), east Alamgirpur (UP, Hindon), west Sutkagen Dor (Baluchistan), south Daimabad.
Trick: "Ravi pe Harappa, Indus pe Mohenjodaro" — the civilization is named after the Indus, but its first site sits on the Ravi.

Life, crops, seals and beliefs

  • Harappans were the first in Eurasia to grow cotton. PYQ The Greeks called it Sindon. CGL+ Main crops: wheat and barley. Rice husk found at Lothal. CGL+
  • Seals were made of local steatite (soft soapstone) and show animals + script → both statements correct. PYQ The script is still undeciphered; it ran right to left. CGL+ Most common animal on seals: the unicorn. CGL+
  • NOT true of Harappans: clear evidence of Sati (none exists). PYQ True: wheat and barley crops, women wore ornaments, worship of a "Pashupati" (proto-Shiva) figure, mother goddess figurines.
  • NOT a feature of Indus cities: use of iron tools. PYQ ×2 They knew copper and bronze only.
  • CGL+ Famous objects: Dancing Girl (bronze) and Priest-King (steatite), both Mohenjodaro. Lothal had a dockyard; Dholavira had a big "signboard" of Harappan letters.

The Vedic library at a glance

  • Yajurveda holds the ritual formulas and step-by-step procedure of sacrifices. PYQ Two branches: Shukla (white) and Krishna (black). CGL+
  • Samaveda = hymns mostly taken from the Rigveda, arranged for singing. PYQ
  • Brahmanas explain the mantras and rituals in prose. PYQ Aitareya (Rigveda), Shatapatha (Yajurveda, the biggest). CGL+
  • Collections of Vedic hymns are called Samhitas. PYQ In the Rigveda, Samhita = a systematic compilation of hymns (in verse, not prose). PYQ
  • The hymns were composed by Rishis and Rishikas (male and female seers). PYQ Female seers: Lopamudra, Ghosha, Apala, Vishvavara. CGL+
  • CGL+ Upavedas: Ayurveda (Rig), Dhanurveda — archery (Yajur), Gandharvaveda — music (Sama), Shilpaveda (Atharva). Dhanurveda is NOT one of the four Vedas.

The Rigveda, piece by piece

  • Rigveda = a collection of hymns composed across ages. PYQ Not Upanishads, not chronicles, not music.
  • 10 Mandalas PYQ; each Mandala is divided into Suktas PYQ ×2; about 1028 hymns PYQ.
  • 'Rik' = a sacred hymn / verse of praise. PYQ (Rita = cosmic order, Saman = melody, Yajna = sacrifice.)
  • The 9th Mandala is entirely about Soma. PYQ
  • The name "Bharata" first appears in the Rigveda, as a tribe. PYQ Bharata king Sudas won the Battle of Ten Kings on the Parushni (Ravi). CGL+
  • CGL+ Gayatri Mantra: Mandala 3, by Vishvamitra, to Savitri (sun). Most hymns: Indra (~250), then Agni (~200). River most named: Sindhu; holiest: Sarasvati (Naditama).

Rigvedic society and polity

  • Jana = a tribe or group of related families — the top political unit. PYQ Smaller units: Vish (clan), Grama (village), Kula (family). CGL+
  • Marriage: monogamy was the general rule, but some princes had many wives. PYQ
  • CGL+ Assemblies: Sabha (council of elders), Samiti (general assembly), Vidatha (oldest), Gana. Women attended Sabha and Vidatha in the Rigvedic age. King's voluntary gift-tax: Bali.
  • Varnas: Vaishya did farming, cattle and trade. PYQ Brahmin–priests, Kshatriya–warriors, Shudra–service. First three were "twice-born" (Dvija). CGL+
  • "Four varnas" and "four stages of life (ashramas)" — both statements are correct. PYQ Ashramas: Brahmacharya → Grihastha → Vanaprastha → Sannyasa. CGL+
  • Languages alongside Sanskrit: Pali and Prakrit (spoken by common people). PYQ
  • CGL+ Later Vedic age (c. 1000–600 BCE): iron (krishna ayas), move to the Ganga plains, Sabha and Samiti lose power, varna becomes rigid.

Buddha's life on one line

  • PYQ Birth year ≈ 563 BCE (option given was 560). PYQ Meditated at Bodh Gaya.

What the Buddha taught

  • Four Noble Truths: suffering exists (dukkha) → it has a cause (samudaya) → it can end (nirodha) → the way (magga = Eightfold Path).
  • Root causes of suffering: Ignorance (avidya) and Attachment (tanha). PYQ
  • Middle Way: neither luxury nor severe penance.
  • Metta = loving-kindness, fellow feeling. PYQ The four Brahmaviharas: Metta, Karuna (compassion), Mudita (joy), Upekkha (equanimity).
  • CGL+ Three Jewels (Triratna): Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha. Texts in Pali: Tripitaka — Sutta, Vinaya, Abhidhamma.
  • Disciples: Ananda (cousin, attendant), Upali (Vinaya expert), Sariputta, Moggallana. Susima was NOT a disciple — he was Ashoka's elder half-brother. PYQ
