Circular motion and rotation

  • Stone in a circle at constant speed: it has an acceleration directed towards the centre (centripetal). Speed is constant but velocity keeps changing, because direction changes. PYQ
  • Uniform circular motion is accelerated motion. Centripetal acceleration = v²/r. If the string breaks, the stone flies off along the tangent. CGL+
  • For a rigid body rotating about an axis, angular velocity ω is the same for all particles. Linear speed v = ωr differs. PYQ
  • Moment of inertia = rotational version of mass (how hard it is to change rotation). Its value depends on the axis and on how mass is spread around that axis. PYQ SI unit kg·m². CGL+
⚠ Answer-key note (moment of inertia)Book says "a tensor quantity, independent of the axis". That cannot be right: moment of inertia always depends on the axis (the book's own explanation says so). About a given axis it is a single number, so the best option is "a scalar, dependent on the axis". In advanced physics the full description is a tensor, but never "independent of the axis".

Relative motion

  • Things you look at seem to move opposite to your own motion (trees seem to run backwards from a moving train).
  • Merry-go-round turning anti-clockwise, watching a tree: book's answer is Right to Left. PYQ
⚠ Answer-key note (merry-go-round)Book says the tree appears from the right and exits to the left. Check it: on an anti-clockwise ride your seat keeps turning to your left, exactly like turning your head left. When you turn left, the scene slides the other way, so a far-off tree enters your view from the left and leaves on the right. By this reasoning the answer should be Left to Right. Remember the book's key, but know the logic.

Physics in sports (asked with motion)

  • CGL+ Four phases of long jump: approach run → take-off → flight → landing.

Force & Laws of Motion

Most asked: Newton's 3rd law (rockets, recoil), pressure = force/area, inertia, friction

Newton's three laws on one card each

First law and inertia

  • "An object stays at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled by a force" = First law of motion (law of inertia). PYQ
  • Inertia = the tendency of an object to resist any change in its state of motion. PYQ
  • Heavy box: hard to start and hard to stop → property of inertia. PYQ Mass is the measure of inertia. CGL+
  • A change in speed or direction means a change in its state of motion. PYQ
  • Unbalanced force → change in speed or direction (✔). Balanced force cannot accelerate a body (✘). So only statement I is right. PYQ
  • CGL+ Three kinds: inertia of rest (passengers fall back when bus starts), of motion (fall forward when it stops), of direction (pushed outward on a turn). Galileo first gave the idea of inertia.

Second law and momentum

  • Momentum p = mv (vector, kg·m/s). Force = rate of change of momentum, so F = ma. Unit: newton (N) = kg·m/s². CGL+
  • Cricketer moves hands back while catching to reduce the force by increasing the time over which momentum changes. PYQ
  • CGL+ Same idea: cushions/sand pits for jumpers, airbags in cars, shock absorbers. Impulse = F × t = change in momentum.
  • Conservation of momentum: with no outside force, total momentum stays the same. Best example: a gun recoils backward when a bullet is fired. PYQ Before firing total momentum = 0, so after firing bullet's forward momentum = gun's backward momentum.

Third law

  • Statement: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. PYQ ×2 Not "opposite but greater" — always equal.
  • Rockets and satellite launch vehicles work on Newton's third law. PYQ ×2 (Also explained by conservation of momentum.)
  • Walking on sand is hard because sand gives way (is displaced), so the ground pushes back less. PYQ
  • CGL+ Action and reaction act on different bodies, so they never cancel each other.

Free-body diagram: a box being pushed

Friction

  • Contact vs non-contact: friction is a contact force; electrostatic force acts from a distance. So only II (friction). PYQ
  • Fluid friction is also called drag, and it depends on the speed of the object relative to the fluid → both I and II correct. PYQ
  • Matchstick lights because friction produces heat. PYQ
  • Disadvantage of friction: wear and tear of materials (also heat and energy loss). PYQ
  • CGL+ Order: static > sliding (kinetic) > rolling. That is why wheels and ball bearings help. Reduce friction with lubricants, polishing, ball bearings; streamlined shapes cut drag (fish, birds, aeroplanes). Increase friction with treaded tyres, spiked shoes.

Thrust and pressure

  • Force acting on unit area = pressure. Formula: Pressure = Force / Area = Thrust / Area. PYQ ×3
  • SI unit of pressure: pascal (Pa) = 1 N/m². PYQ (Newton = force, watt = power, ampere = current.)
  • Area reduced, force same → pressure increases. PYQ
  • We are not crushed by air because the pressure inside our body balances the outside pressure. PYQ
  • CGL+ Thrust = force acting perpendicular to a surface (unit N). Atmospheric pressure at sea level ≈ 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa = 76 cm of mercury. It falls as you go up.
  • CGL+ Liquid pressure = hρg: grows with depth, so dams are thicker at the bottom.

One odd one out (sports rule)

Field hockey: players may hit the ball only with the flat (left) face of the stick, for better control and reduced injury risk. PYQ

Check yourself

Liquid pressure increases with depth (P = hρg).

Which is larger: static or rolling friction?

What measures inertia?

Mass. More mass, more inertia.

Universal law of gravitation

F = G m₁m₂ / r² Every mass attracts every other mass. Force ∝ product of masses, ∝ 1/(distance)². G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (a universal constant). CGL+ Given by Newton.
  • Force that runs planets, stars and galaxies: gravitational force (infinite range, always attractive). PYQ
  • The law explains both the motion of planets round the Sun and the tides due to Moon and Sun. PYQ
  • Under gravity alone, an orbiting body keeps angular momentum and mechanical energy conserved (central force → no torque; gravity is a conservative force). PYQ
  • CGL+ Four fundamental forces, strongest to weakest: strong nuclear > electromagnetic > weak nuclear > gravitational.

Heat, Gases & Measurement

Boiling point and pressure

  • Pressure cooker: I ✘ (it raises pressure, not lowers), II ✔ (water boils above 100 °C), III ✔ (hot steam passes heat fast) → only II and III. PYQ
  • CGL+ More pressure → higher boiling point (cooker ≈ 120 °C). Less pressure → lower boiling point, so food cooks slowly on hills.
  • Copper-bottom utensils: copper is a good conductor of heat, so heat spreads fast and evenly. PYQ
  • CGL+ Three ways heat travels: conduction (solids), convection (liquids, gases; sea breeze), radiation (no medium needed; Sun's heat).

Latent heat: why steam burns more

  • Steam at 100 °C burns worse than water at 100 °C because steam carries extra latent heat of vaporisation; it gives this out when it condenses on skin. PYQ
  • CGL+ Latent heat: fusion of ice ≈ 334 J/g (80 cal/g), vaporisation of water ≈ 2260 J/g (540 cal/g). Water has a very high specific heat (≈ 4186 J/kg·K), so it is used in hot-water bags and car radiators.
  • CGL+ Water is densest at 4 °C (anomalous expansion) — lakes freeze from the top, fish survive below.
  • CGL+ Scales: K = °C + 273; °F = (9/5)°C + 32; −40 is the same on °C and °F. Normal body temperature 37 °C = 98.6 °F.

Ideal gas and thermodynamics

  • Ideal gas: molecules have negligible volume ✔, no attraction between them (so "strong attraction" ✘), collisions perfectly elastic ✔ → 1 and 3 only. PYQ
  • CGL+ Real gases act most like ideal gases at high temperature and low pressure. Ideal gas equation: PV = nRT.
  • Heat-engine statements: ideal-engine efficiency depends only on reservoir temperatures ✔; heat goes cold → hot only with outside work ✔; "irreversible expansion gives more work than quasi-static" ✘ (reversible gives the most) → 2 and 3 only. PYQ

Photoelectric effect

  • Statements: KE rises with intensity ✘ (KE depends on frequency); shows particle nature of light ✔; threshold frequency exists ✔ → 2 and 3 only. PYQ
  • CGL+ More intensity → more electrons, not faster ones. Photon energy E = hν (h = Planck's constant). Used in solar cells, automatic doors, light meters.
  • CGL+ Light shows both natures: interference/diffraction (wave), photoelectric effect (particle) — "wave–particle duality".
