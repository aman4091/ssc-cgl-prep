Solid, liquid, gas: the particle picture

  • Forces of attraction are maximum in solids and minimum in gases. PYQ
  • Gases: particles move randomly at high speed, and gases are highly compressible → both statements correct. PYQ That is why LPG and CNG fit in cylinders. CGL+
  • Water is written as H₂O — two hydrogen atoms, one oxygen atom. PYQ (CO₂ = carbon dioxide, O₂ = oxygen, NaCl = common salt.)
  • CGL+ Diffusion is fastest in gases, slowest in solids. The 4th and 5th states of matter: plasma (sun, stars, neon signs) and Bose–Einstein condensate (super-cooled gas; S.N. Bose).

What decides the state? PYQ ×2 Both pressure and temperature. More pressure and less heat push particles together (towards liquid / solid); more heat and less pressure push them apart (towards gas). Weight, density or viscosity do not decide it. CGL+ Gases are liquefied by high pressure + low temperature.

Latent heat: why the thermometer stops

  • While a pure substance melts, its temperature stays constant. PYQ
  • Heat to turn 1 kg of solid into liquid at its melting point = latent heat of fusion ("latent" = hidden). PYQ
  • CGL+ Steam at 100 °C burns more than water at 100 °C, because steam carries the extra latent heat of vaporisation.
  • CGL+ Kelvin = °C + 273. −40 is the same on the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales.

Evaporation and cooling

  • Factors that change the rate of evaporation: surface area, temperature, humidity, wind speed. Buoyancy is NOT one of them. PYQ
  • Rate goes up with more area, more heat, more wind; goes down with more humidity. CGL+
  • Evaporation causes cooling: acetone on the palm, and water sprinkled on a hot roof — both I and II. PYQ Same reason: sweating cools us; water in an earthen pot (matka) stays cool. CGL+

Check yourself

Gas straight to solid — what is it called?

Deposition. Solid straight to gas is sublimation.

Which two factors decide whether something is solid, liquid or gas?

Pressure and temperature.

Why does the temperature stay at 0 °C while ice melts?

The heat goes into breaking particle attraction (latent heat of fusion), not into raising temperature.

Is buoyancy a factor in evaporation?

No. Area, temperature, humidity and wind are.

Atoms and radioactivity
