Rotation vs revolution

WhatEarth spins on its own axisEarth goes round the Sun
Time~24 h (23 h 56 m 4 s)365¼ days
DirectionWest → East (anticlockwise seen from above the North Pole)Also anticlockwise; orbit is an ellipse
ResultDay and night PYQ; time zones PYQ; Sun seems to rise in the east because Earth turns west to east PYQ; Coriolis forceOne year and the cycle of seasons (together with the tilt) PYQ
Trick: Rotation = Roz ka kaam (daily: day-night, time). Revolution = Round the year (year, seasons).
  • Earth turns 360° in 24 h → 15° per hour, 1° = 4 minutes. That is why places on different longitudes have different local times. CGL+
  • IST is fixed on 82.5° E (near Mirzapur, UP) = GMT + 5:30. Prime Meridian (0°) passes Greenwich; International Date Line ≈ 180°. CGL+
  • The line that separates the lit half from the dark half = circle of illumination. CGL+
  • Venus and Uranus spin the other way (east to west). CGL+ In India, the first sunrise is in Dong, Arunachal Pradesh; the west end is Guhar Moti, Gujarat. The two are about 2 hours apart in sun time.
  • Extra ¼ day each year → one extra day every 4 years = leap year (29 February). CGL+

The tilted axis: seasons and long polar days

  • The axis leans 23.4° (often written 23.5°) from the upright. PYQ So it makes 66.5° with the orbit plane. CGL+
  • Seasons come from the tilt of the axis, not from the Earth–Sun distance. PYQ
  • Poles get months of non-stop day or night because of the axial tilt. PYQ Roughly 6 months day, 6 months night. Norway = "Land of the Midnight Sun". CGL+
  • Climates differ under the same Sun because of the angle of sunlight: straight rays at the equator, slanting rays near the poles spread the same heat over more ground. PYQ
  • In June the Sun is over the Tropic of Cancer, so the further north a place is, the longer its day. Among Hyderabad, Chennai, Bhopal and Delhi, Delhi (northernmost) has the longest June day. PYQ In December the order flips.
  • The axis always points to the Pole Star (parallelism of the axis). CGL+

Shape of the Earth and its name

  • The geoid ("Earth-shaped") is the shape the sea surface would take under gravity alone. It reflects global gravity variations; it is not a perfect sphere and not the land surface. PYQ
  • Earth is an oblate spheroid: flat at the poles, bulging at the equator. Equatorial diameter (~12,756 km) > polar (~12,714 km). Gravity is a little stronger at the poles. CGL+
  • "Earth" comes from Old English and Germanic words for ground or soil. PYQ It is the only planet not named after a Greek or Roman god.
  • CGL+ Blue Planet (about 71% water) · 3rd from the Sun · 5th largest · densest planet · one Moon · Venus = "Earth's twin" (similar size).

LayerState / natureExam point

MantleSolid but can flow slowly; asthenosphere is semi-moltenThickest layer PYQ. Plates move on the asthenosphere; magma comes from here. CGL+

  • Core = NIFE: Nickel + Iron (Fe = ferrum). PYQ Crust = SIAL (silica + aluminium); below it SIMA (silica + magnesium). Terms by Eduard Suess. CGL+
  • Isostasy = gravitational balance between crust and mantle; blocks "float" according to their thickness and density, like ice on water. PYQ Mountains float high with deep roots; dense ocean floor sits low. Word given by C.E. Dutton; theories by Pratt and Airy. CGL+
Trick: Bahar se andar: SIAL → SIMA → NIFE. Boundaries in order: M-G-L = Moho, Gutenberg, Lehmann ("Mera Ghar Lucknow").

Earthquake waves

  • Waves similar to sound that go through gas, liquid and solid = P-waves. PYQ
  • Fastest waves = Primary waves, always arriving first. PYQ
  • CGL+ Point inside the Earth where the quake starts = focus (hypocentre); point right above it = epicentre. Recorded by a seismograph; magnitude on the Richter scale. Lines of equal intensity = isoseismal lines.

Check yourself

Which motion gives time zones: rotation or revolution?

Rotation (15° of longitude per hour).

Which layer is liquid, and which is the thickest?

Outer core is liquid. Mantle is the thickest.

Earth is closest to the Sun in which month?

January (perihelion, ~3 Jan). So distance does not cause seasons; the tilt does.

Which earthquake waves cannot cross the outer core?

S-waves (they travel only through solids).

What air is made of

  • Dry air: Nitrogen 78.1%, Oxygen 20.9%. PYQ Argon (~0.93%) is third and the commonest noble gas in air; CO₂ ~0.04%. CGL+
  • Trace gases: Neon ~0.0018%, Helium ~0.0005%, Ozone ~0.00006%. PYQ Noble gases in air: Argon > Neon > Helium > Krypton > Xenon.
  • CGL+ Ozone layer protects us from UV; measured in Dobson Units. Montreal Protocol 1987; 16 September = World Ozone Day.

Layers of the atmosphere

  • Statement test on the ionosphere: the true statement was that it reflects radio waves for long-distance communication → answer Only 2. PYQ
  • CGL+ Temperature falls about 6.5°C per km in the troposphere (normal lapse rate). The boundary on top of it = tropopause.

Volcanoes can cool the planet

Krakatoa mechanism PYQ A huge eruption throws sulphur gas into the stratosphere. It forms tiny droplets (aerosols) that bounce sunlight back to space → volcanic aerosols in the stratosphere reflect solar radiation and cause global cooling. Krakatoa (1883) is in the Sunda Strait, Indonesia, between Java and Sumatra.

CGL+ Tambora (Indonesia, 1815) → 1816 "Year without a Summer". Pinatubo (Philippines, 1991) cooled the globe ~0.5°C. Most active volcanoes lie on the Pacific Ring of Fire.

Ocean circulation

⚠ Answer-key note The official key for the "Thermohaline Circulation" question marked the pull of the Moon and Sun causing tides. That is not the science. The conveyor belt is driven by differences in density caused by temperature (thermo) and salinity (haline). PYQ Cold, salty water is heavy and sinks, mainly in the North Atlantic near Greenland; one full loop takes about 1,000 years. Tides are a different thing, caused mostly by the Moon's gravity.

Wearing down the land

  • Slow wearing away of the surface by wind, water and ice = erosion. PYQ Weathering breaks rock in place; erosion breaks and carries it away. CGL+
  • CGL+ Running water is the strongest agent. Wind makes mushroom rocks and sand dunes; glaciers make cirques, moraines and U-shaped valleys. Gully erosion makes the Chambal ravines.
  • Avalanches are most likely when snow begins to melt rapidly on slopes (wet heavy snow slides), or when fresh heavy snow overloads a slope. PYQ Most start on 30°–45° slopes. A slow-moving glacier is not an avalanche; a slide of rock and soil is a landslide.

Climate Change & Green Ideas

Selection Post Phase-XIV 2026 · 16–26 Sep · Rio 1992, CBDR, geoengineering (twice), Green Credit Programme (twice)

Milestones on one line

  • Agenda 21 (Rio 1992) reflected a global commitment to link environment with development = sustainable development. PYQ Non-binding; "21" = 21st century. CGL+
  • CBDR-RC (Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities): developed nations must take the lead, because of their past emissions and greater resources. PYQ
  • CGL+ UNFCCC: signed 1992, in force 1994, secretariat Bonn. Kyoto put binding cuts only on developed (Annex I) countries. Paris: countries file NDCs. India: Net Zero by 2070; 50% of power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030, reached in 2025.

Ideas and terms

  • Deep Ecology (Arne Naess, Norway, 1973) asks for a shift from anthropocentrism to biocentrism, valuing all life regardless of its use to humans. PYQ "Shallow ecology" protects nature only for human benefit. CGL+
  • Great Acceleration: the sharp post-1950 rise in human activity; clearest in global primary energy use and atmospheric CO₂. PYQ
  • CGL+ Anthropocene = proposed "human age" epoch (Paul Crutzen); geologists rejected it as a formal epoch in 2024. We are officially in the Holocene (~11,700 years). CO₂ is above 420 ppm (pre-industrial ~280).
  • India's rank in the Climate Risk Index for 2019 (Germanwatch, 2021 edition): 7th most affected. PYQ Germanwatch also publishes the CCPI. NAPCC (2008) has 8 missions. CGL+

Geoengineering

  • Geoengineering = large-scale intervention in Earth's climate system to cool the planet. PYQ
  • Primary goal (key): remove greenhouse gases. PYQ
⚠ Answer-key note · goal of geoengineering "Remove greenhouse gases" fits only the CDR half. SRM cools the planet by reflecting sunlight and removes no gas. Among the given options (boost fossil fuels, create deserts, remove ocean oxygen) the key's choice is the only sensible one, so mark it.

Green Credit Programme

What, who, when Rewards environmentally positive actions PYQ with tradable green credits. One stated aim: restore degraded lands PYQ. Notified October 2023 by MoEFCC under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; run by ICFRE, Dehradun. Activities: tree planting, water saving, sustainable farming, waste management, mangroves. Linked to Mission LiFE (launched October 2022, Kevadia). Green credits are not carbon credits (those count only greenhouse gases).
