What Economics Is: Scarcity, Demand & Elasticity

Selection Post Phase-XIV 2026 · 16–26 Sep · most asked: Robbins vs Marshall, Giffen / inferior goods, elasticity formula

Four definitions of economics

  • What sets economics apart: it studies how scarce resources are allocated among competing uses. PYQ
  • Economics is "the study of choice" because scarce resources must be shared among competing uses. PYQ Chain: scarcity → choice → opportunity cost.
  • Marshall's focus, unlike Robbins: human welfare and the material requisites of well-being. PYQ Robbins called it "classificatory". CGL+
  • Opportunity cost = the next best alternative given up. PYQ Also called alternative or transfer cost. The PPC (production possibility curve) shows it and is concave to the origin. Sunk cost = money already spent that cannot come back. CGL+
  • CGL+ Richard Thaler won the 2017 Economics Nobel for behavioural economics ("nudge") — people are not always fully rational.

Economic systems and micro vs macro

SystemWho answers what / how / for whomExample
CapitalistMarket pricesUSA
SocialistGovernmentFormer USSR
Mixed PYQGovernment and market togetherIndia (Industrial Policy Resolutions 1948, 1956) CGL+
  • Microeconomics deals with individual economic units — one consumer, one firm, one market. PYQ Macro = whole economy: national income, inflation, budget.
  • CGL+ Words "micro" and "macro" first used by Ragnar Frisch (1933). Keynes = father of macroeconomics (General Theory, 1936).

The demand curve

  • A demand curve links price and quantity of a good. PYQ It slopes down; a supply curve slopes up; where they cross = equilibrium price. CGL+
  • NOT an exception to the law of demand: substitute goods (tea–coffee obey the law). PYQ
  • Real exceptions: Giffen goods, Veblen / conspicuous goods (Thorstein Veblen; status items), expectation of future price rise, speculation (gold, shares). CGL+
  • CGL+ Substitutes have positive cross elasticity; complements (car and petrol) have negative cross elasticity.

Elasticity and kinds of goods

Price elasticity of demand PYQ ×2 Ed = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price. It measures how strongly demand responds to price changes. Ed > 1 elastic (luxuries, goods with many substitutes); Ed < 1 inelastic (salt, medicines); Ed = 0 vertical curve; Ed = ∞ horizontal curve. CGL+
  • Negative income elasticity → inferior good. PYQ
  • Example of a Giffen good: bajra (millet) — a cheap staple; when its price rises, poor families drop costlier food and buy even more of it. PYQ Named after Sir Robert Giffen; classic case: potatoes in the Irish famine. CGL+

CGL+ HDI was built by Mahbub ul Haq (1990, UNDP): health, education, standard of living.

Why is a death rate a "social" fact and not just personal data? Because it shows patterns shaped by society. PYQ CGL+ Émile Durkheim ("Suicide", 1897) called these social facts. From GDP to National Income

  • Total value of final goods and services made inside a country: GDP. PYQ Estimated by NSO (MoSPI). CGL+
  • The gap between GNP and GDP is income residents earn from investment (factors) abroad — net factor income from abroad. PYQ For India NFIA is negative, so GNP < GDP. CGL+
  • Depreciation (consumption of fixed capital) is taken from GDP to get NDP. PYQ
  • National income = total value of goods and services produced by a country (technically NNP at factor cost). PYQ
  • Payments with nothing given back (pensions, scholarships) = transfer payments. PYQ Not counted in national income; part of personal income. DBT started 1 January 2013; JAM = Jan Dhan, Aadhaar, Mobile. CGL+
  • Potential GDP minus actual GDP = output gap. PYQ Negative gap = idle capacity; positive gap = overheating, inflation risk. CGL+
National income history CGL+ First estimate: Dadabhai Naoroji (1867–68, "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India"). First scientific estimate: V.K.R.V. Rao (1931). National Income Committee 1949: P.C. Mahalanobis. Three methods: product (value added), income, expenditure.

  • Reform for fiscal discipline after 1991: the FRBM Act (2003, in force July 2004). PYQ Original target: fiscal deficit 3% of GDP, zero revenue deficit; escape clause for war, calamity, deep slowdown. CGL+

  • A deficit budget suits an economic depression or recession — extra spending lifts demand (Keynes, after the Great Depression 1929–33). PYQ High inflation calls for a surplus budget. CGL+
  • Policy tool that moves aggregate demand through government spending: fiscal policy. PYQ Monetary policy is RBI's (repo, CRR, SLR, OMO); the MPC has 6 members and a 4% ± 2% inflation target. CGL+

Taxes

  • Tax that keeps a fixed percentage of income and acts as an automatic stabiliser (per the key): proportional tax. PYQ
⚠ Answer-key noteThe key says "proportional tax", and that fits the question's words "fixed percentage of income". But in standard textbooks the classic automatic stabiliser is the progressive income tax — its collection rises faster than income in a boom and falls faster in a slump. A proportional tax stabilises only weakly. In the exam, follow the wording: "fixed percentage" → proportional.

The 1991 Reforms (LPG)

From crisis to reform

  • India borrowed from the World Bank and the IMF in the 1991 crisis. PYQ Gold was pledged with the Bank of England and Bank of Japan; both IMF and World Bank came from Bretton Woods (1944), HQ Washington DC. CGL+
  • New Economic Policy announced in 1991. PYQ The 24 July 1991 Budget is called historic. CGL+
  • Three parts: Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation. PYQ
  • "Father of Indian Economic Reforms", 9th PM: P.V. Narasimha Rao. PYQ First PM from South India; Bharat Ratna (posthumous) in 2024; Look East Policy (1991). Manmohan Singh was PM 2004–14 and died in December 2024. CGL+

Industry: the end of the Licence Raj

  • Pre-1991 web of licences, quotas and permits = Licence Raj PYQ; it means the industrial licensing system. PYQ C. Rajagopalachari popularised "Licence-Permit-Quota Raj"; its legal base was the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act 1951. CGL+
  • NIP 1991's structural change: licensing abolished for all but a few industries. PYQ
  • Still needs a licence: hazardous chemicals PYQ — also alcoholic drinks, tobacco products, defence/aerospace electronics, industrial explosives. CGL+
  • CGL+ Reserved for the public sector: 17 industries (1956) → 8 (1991) → now only atomic energy and railway operations. MRTP Act 1969 was replaced by the Competition Act 2002 (CCI, 2003).

Trade and the rupee

  • FDI equity limits were increased (51% automatic approval in 34 high-priority industries). PYQ CGL+ FIPB 1991, abolished 2017; FEMA 1999 replaced FERA 1973.
  • Exchange rate: shift to a market-determined exchange rate. PYQ CGL+ LERMS 1992 (dual rate) → unified market rate March 1993 → "managed float". Rupee convertible on current account in 1994; capital account still partly.
  • Dismantled to boost competition: quantitative restrictions on imports. PYQ CGL+ QRs on consumer goods fully gone by April 2001 (WTO). WTO born 1 January 1995, India a founding member. Foreign Trade Policy 2023 aims at $2 trillion exports by 2030.

Who gained

  • Sector that gained most from LPG: services PYQ; it also drove GDP growth after 1991. PYQ
  • CGL+ Rough GVA shares: services ~55%, industry ~27%, agriculture ~18% — yet agriculture employs the most workers (~45%). India's growth is "service-led" (China's is manufacturing-led). Make in India (2014) aims at 25% manufacturing share; PLI schemes (2020) for 14 sectors.
  • A firm hiring regular services from outside, often abroad = outsourcing. PYQ BPO = business process, KPO = knowledge process. CGL+
  • India became an outsourcing hub thanks to low wages and skilled manpower. PYQ CGL+ Time-zone gap gave Western firms a 24-hour cycle; NASSCOM (1988), STPI (1991); Bengaluru = "Silicon Valley of India".
