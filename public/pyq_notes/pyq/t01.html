More from 1919 and 1935 CGL+

Federalism: what kind of federation is India?

  • Dividing power between Union and States = federal system PYQ; power is divided between Centre and States PYQ.
  • True statement: the Union has overriding authority. PYQ States are not sovereign and cannot secede.
  • "Quasi-federal" / "federal in form, unitary in spirit" because of strong centralising powers of the Union. PYQ The term quasi-federal is from K.C. Wheare. CGL+
  • Dual citizenship is NOT a feature of Indian federalism (India has single citizenship). PYQ
  • Indian federalism is biased towards the Centre ✔; the word "federal" is used in the Constitution ✘. So only 1. PYQ Art 1 says "Union of States".
  • Supreme law of India: the Constitution. PYQ
  • Basic structure doctrine was created by the Supreme Court PYQ (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973); the phrase is not written in Art 368.
  • Body that can change the Union–State distribution of powers: Parliament PYQ, by special majority plus ratification by half the States (Art 368). CGL+

Articles 1 to 4

  • Admission or establishment of new States: Article 2. PYQ
  • Who can alter state boundaries or divide a State: Parliament (Art 3). PYQ ×2 The President only recommends the bill and refers it to the State legislature.

How the map of States changed

  • Andhra, the first linguistic State, came after the death of Potti Sriramulu (Dec 1952, after a 56-day fast). PYQ Andhra formed on 1 Oct 1953.
  • Correct pair: Chhattisgarh – 2000. PYQ (Uttarakhand and Jharkhand also 2000; Telangana 2014.)
  • CGL+ Commissions: Dhar Commission (1948) rejected language as a basis; JVP Committee (1948: Jawaharlal, Vallabhbhai, Pattabhi Sitaramayya); Fazl Ali Commission (1953) → States Reorganisation Act 1956.

Jammu & Kashmir and Art 370

Under Art 370, J&K had all of the above PYQ: its own Constitution, its own flag, and control of its affairs except defence, foreign affairs and communications. CGL+ Art 370 was a "temporary provision" in Part XXI; it was made inoperative in August 2019 and J&K was split into two UTs (J&K and Ladakh) from 31 Oct 2019.

Union Territories and citizenship CGL+

  • UTs are in Part VIII (Art 239–242), run by the President through an administrator. Delhi has a legislature under Art 239AA (69th Amdt, 1991); Puducherry under Art 239A.
  • Citizenship: Part II, Art 5–11; law is the Citizenship Act 1955. Ways to get it: birth, descent, registration, naturalisation, incorporation of territory. Ways to lose it: renunciation, termination, deprivation.
  • India has single citizenship (from the UK).
  • Foreigners' Tribunals (Assam) decide whether a person excluded from the NRC is a citizen: they verify citizenship claims of those left out of the NRC. PYQ

The basics in 4 lines

  • Fundamental Rights are in Part III, Articles 12 to 35. PYQ ×2
  • Part III is called the "Magna Carta of India" (Magna Carta = 1215 charter of King John, England). PYQ

The five writs

PYQ Lower court going beyond its jurisdiction, case still running → Prohibition. Already decided → Certiorari. PYQ Matching: Habeas Corpus–release, Mandamus–duty, Certiorari–quash, Quo Warranto–public office.

Right to Property: the favourite trap

Removed from FRs by the 44th Amendment, 1978 PYQ ×2 Old place: Art 19(1)(f) + Art 31. New place: Art 300A, Part XII. Now a constitutional/legal right: "No person shall be deprived of property save by authority of law". Enforced through Art 226 (High Court), not Art 32.

Three tiers of Panchayati Raj

  • Village-level tier: Gram Panchayat. PYQ
  • Three-tier structure first recommended by the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957). PYQ First started in Nagaur, Rajasthan, 2 Oct 1959. CGL+
  • Municipalities = 12th Schedule (74th Amendment, 18 subjects). PYQ Panchayats = 11th Schedule (73rd, 29 subjects). CGL+

73rd vs 74th (1992) CGL+73rd: Part IX, Art 243–243O, 11th Schedule, rural. 74th: Part IX-A, Art 243P–243ZG, 12th Schedule, urban. Both: 5-year term, 1/3 seats for women, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission. Minimum age to contest: 21.
