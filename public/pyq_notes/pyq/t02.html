Planning in 5 lines

  • Planning Commission set up in 1950 (15 March, by a Cabinet resolution, not by the Constitution). PYQ Chairman = Prime Minister (Nehru first). CGL+
  • Its main job: to formulate and implement Five Year Plans. PYQ
  • Replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015. The 12th Plan (2012–17) was the last plan. CGL+
  • Architect of Indian planning = P.C. Mahalanobis (founder of the Indian Statistical Institute, father of Indian statistics). PYQ ×2
  • M. Visvesvaraya wrote Planned Economy for India (1934) — the first push for planning. Bombay Plan (1944) came from industrialists. CGL+
  • Plans are drafted, debated and changed: Indian planning shows modifying drafts based on criticism (public and expert feedback). PYQ

The policy timeline

IPR 1948 and IPR 1956

  • The first industrial policy of independent India came in 1948 (6 April). PYQ It aimed at promoting the public sector and set up a mixed economy. PYQ Four categories; state monopoly on arms, atomic energy, railways. CGL+
  • IPR 1956 = "Economic Constitution of India". CGL+ Central theme: state control of the commanding heights of the economy PYQ; key feature: expansion of the public sector PYQ; main objective: growth with equity (socialist pattern of society) PYQ.
  • It did not aim at private-sector dominance. PYQ ×2
  • Even expanding an existing private factory needed a licence — to keep production under government control. PYQ

Why the public sector got the big role (1947–1990)

License Raj: what went wrong

The 6 License Raj answers PYQ ×6

The budget tree

  • Salaries and pensions of govt employees → revenue expenditure (both). PYQ ×2 Salaries, pensions, defence services → revenue expenditure. PYQ
  • Interest on government debt → revenue expenditure. PYQ
  • Spending for the normal running of departments → revenue expenditure. PYQ
  • NOT capital expenditure: subsidies (land, buildings, shares are capital). PYQ
  • "Capital expenditure creates no asset" ✘; "buying machinery is capital expenditure" ✔ → only II. PYQ
  • Loans create a liability, sale of assets does not. PYQ Borrowing year after year → accumulation of debt. PYQ
  • Subsidies aim at welfare; defence is "committed" spending with little room to cut → both correct. PYQ
  • Traditional (Keynesian) view: tax cuts + budget deficit → consumers spend more. PYQ
Deficits in one line each CGL+ Revenue deficit = revenue exp − revenue receipts. Fiscal deficit = total exp − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts) = total borrowing. Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest payments. By 1990–91 the gross fiscal deficit had climbed to about 10% of GDP PYQ — one cause of the 1991 crisis.

The core ideas

  • Scarcity = limited resources, unlimited wants. PYQ Scarcity definition of economics: Lionel Robbins (1932). PYQ

  • Adam Smith's "invisible hand": market balance reached through self-interest. PYQ CGL+ Smith = father of economics; Wealth of Nations, 1776.

  • Three central problems: what, how, for whom. "What to produce" = choosing between capital and consumer goods. PYQ
  • Indifference curve is also called the iso-utility curve. PYQ (Phillips curve = inflation vs unemployment. CGL+)

Balance of Payments in one picture

The Balance of Payments (BoP) is the record of all money flows between India and the rest of the world in a year. It has two big accounts.

The 1991 crisis: what went wrong

  • It was an external debt / BoP crisis: India could not repay its foreign borrowings. PYQ ×2

Demand meets supply

  • The curve linking price and quantity demanded is the demand curve PYQ; it is the graph of the demand function. PYQ

Law of demand

  • As price rises, quantity demanded falls, other things equal (ceteris paribus). PYQ CGL+ Stated by Alfred Marshall.
