Planning & the Five Year Plans (1950–1990)

Steno 2026 · 9–15 Sep · most asked: First Plan priority, Mahalanobis / Second Plan, import substitution, mixed economy

Who planned, and from where the idea came

  • The Planning Commission was set up on 15 March 1950 by a Cabinet resolution. PYQ It was neither constitutional nor statutory. CGL+
  • Chairman: the PM, Jawaharlal Nehru. First Deputy Chairman: Gulzarilal Nanda. PYQ Nanda was also interim PM twice (1964, 1966).
  • PM for most of the early plans (1951–64): Jawaharlal Nehru. PYQ He called dams and steel plants the "temples of modern India".
  • Five-Year Plans were copied from the former Soviet Union (USSR). PYQ Its first plan began in 1928. CGL+
  • Replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015. First Vice-Chairman: Arvind Panagariya. CGL+
  • CGL+ Earlier plans: M. Visvesvaraya's "Planned Economy for India" (1934), Bombay Plan 1944 (industrialists), Gandhian Plan 1944 (S.N. Agarwal), People's Plan 1945 (M.N. Roy), Sarvodaya Plan 1950 (J.P. Narayan). National Development Council: 1952.

The Mahalanobis model (asked 3 times)

  • Basis of the Second Plan. Put investment in heavy industry and capital goods through state investment. PYQ
  • Why capital goods ("machines that make machines")? To build internal capacity for self-sustaining growth, even if consumption and jobs suffer in the short run. PYQ
  • Long-term effect: a strong industrial base, but neglect of consumer goods and inefficient public sector units. PYQ
  • CGL+ P.C. Mahalanobis founded the Indian Statistical Institute (Kolkata, 1931). 29 June, his birthday, is Statistics Day.

The Nehru-era model: mixed economy and the state on top

  • Model chosen after Independence: Mixed economy — public and private sectors side by side. PYQ
  • "Commanding heights" = state ownership of key sectors like steel, coal and infrastructure. PYQ Phrase first used by Lenin.
  • "Socialist pattern of society" (Avadi Congress session, 1955) showed up as extensive state control over key industries and infrastructure. PYQ
  • Self-reliance meant cutting dependence on foreign technology, capital and imports by building Indian industry, within a mixed economy. PYQ It was not total isolation.
  • IPR 1956 brought a mixed economy with clearly split public and private domains and state control of key industries. PYQ
  • CGL+ First industrial policy: IPR 1948. Licensing law: Industries (Development and Regulation) Act 1951. MRTP Act 1969.

Import substitution, licences and the PSUs

  • Import substitution = make at home what you used to import. Main aim: protect domestic industries from foreign competition. PYQ
  • Tool used till the 1980s: import substitution and tariffs (plus quotas and licences). PYQ
  • Licence-Permit Raj side effect: big firms faced no rivals, so less incentive to upgrade technology and be efficient. PYQ
  • PSE problem beyond losses (1947–90): bureaucratic rigidity and political interference. PYQ
  • Imports in the 1950s–60s: foodgrains, capital goods and machinery. PYQ Food came from the US under PL-480 ("ship-to-mouth" years). CGL+
  • Late 1980s shift (Rajiv Gandhi): selective export promotion and partial liberalisation. PYQ
  • Sector whose GDP share grew most in 1950–90: Industry (about 13% → 25%). PYQ Agriculture's share fell. After 1991, services became the biggest. CGL+
"Hindu Rate of Growth" PYQ Means a long phase of low growth, about 3–3.5% a year (1950s–1980s), caused by heavy state control, red tape and little room for private firms. Coined by economist Raj Krishna (1978). It has nothing to do with religion. CGL+

The 1991 Reforms: Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation

Steno 2026 · 9–15 Sep · most asked: which step is L, P or G; IMF conditions; Narasimham Committee; convertibility; VAT → GST

From crisis to reform

  • Institution that helped in 1991 with reform conditions: International Monetary Fund (IMF). PYQ
  • Role of the IMF and World Bank: a structural adjustment programme as the condition for emergency money. PYQ Not unconditional aid.
  • NEP 1991 fiscal deficit target: 3–4% of GDP (down from about 8.4%). PYQ
  • Forex reserves after 1991: reached historic highs in later decades (over $700 bn in 2024). PYQ

Foreign money: FDI, FERA → FEMA, convertibility

  • Policy that let foreigners own up to 51% in priority sectors: FDI reforms. PYQ FERA's old cap was 40%.
  • Main aim of opening FDI: fill the savings–investment gap and bring technology and management know-how. PYQ
  • FERA 1973 treated foreign exchange as scarce and made even small breaches criminal. PYQ Replaced by the civil law FEMA 1999 (in force 1 June 2000). CGL+
  • Current account convertibility (August 1994): rupee freely convertible for trade in goods, services and remittances, without exposing India to risky capital flows. PYQ
  • Reform that directly made exports more competitive: convertibility on current account. PYQ
  • CGL+ Capital account convertibility is still only partial — Tarapore Committee (1997, 2006). Market exchange rate: LERMS 1992.
Statement question PYQ 1. Import licensing removed for most items — ✔ · 2. Devaluation made imports cheaper — ✘ (devaluation makes imports costlier and exports cheaper) · 3. FDI via automatic route — ✔. Answer: Only 1 and 3.

Banks and taxes

  • Sector that saw new private Indian and foreign players after 1991: Banking. PYQ (HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis, IndusInd from 1994.)
  • Narasimham Committee (1991): cut CRR and SLR so more credit is available. PYQ Also prudential norms, free interest rates. Second committee: 1998. CGL+
  • Chelliah Committee (Tax Reforms Committee, 1991): wider tax base, fewer exemptions, simpler and lower rates. PYQ
  • VAT (states, 1 April 2005; Haryana first in 2003): tax at many stages but only on value added, with input tax credit — ends the cascading "tax on tax". PYQ
  • Fiscal reform that did most for tax efficiency and base: VAT and then GST. PYQ GST: 101st Amendment (2016), launched 1 July 2017; GST Council under Art 279A. CGL+

After 1991: second generation, and the side effects

  • Second-generation reforms: deeper changes in governance, labour laws, financial regulation and infrastructure. PYQ Examples: FRBM Act 2003, Electricity Act 2003, insurance opened (IRDA 1999). CGL+
  • Bad effect of globalisation on farmers: cheaper imported farm goods and volatile world prices. PYQ
  • Jobless growth: growth led by capital-intensive industry and services that create few jobs (low employment elasticity). PYQ

Macro vs micro, GDP vs GNP

  • Not a macroeconomic aggregate: market supply of rice (one good = micro). PYQ Aggregate demand, GDP, price level are macro.
  • Value of all final goods and services produced within the borders: GDP. PYQ NSDP = state level; CPI = retail inflation.

PYQ Remittances in, but larger profit repatriation out: GDP will exceed GNP, because net factor income from abroad is negative.

  • Current account surplus means receipts exceed payments; the key read it as exports > imports. PYQ In Q4 FY25 (Jan–Mar 2025) India had a small surplus of about $13.5 bn (1.3% of GDP), thanks to services exports and remittances; goods trade was still in deficit.
  • HDI (UNDP, 1990, Mahbub ul Haq with Amartya Sen) has 3 parts: life expectancy, education, per-capita income (GNI, PPP). Not a part: pollution levels. PYQ
  • Dependency ratio counts ages 0–14 and 65+ as dependents, against 15–64. PYQ A falling ratio = demographic dividend. CGL+

Fiscal tools vs monetary tools

  • Fiscal policy tool to move aggregate demand: taxation and government spending. PYQ Run by the government (Finance Ministry).
  • Repo rate, SLR, CRR, bank rate = monetary tools of the RBI. CGL+ Inflation target: 4% ±2%, set by a 6-member Monetary Policy Committee (2016).
  • Reverse repo in times of extra liquidity: RBI absorbs surplus money by paying interest on what banks park with it, which discourages excess lending. PYQ

The President's vetoes

⚠ Answer-key note: absolute vetoThe key printed "C / D". The best answer is C — a Money Bill PYQ: the President cannot return it (no suspensive veto), so the only choices are assent or refusal. A reserved state bill (option D) can still be sent back to the state legislature under Art 201, so it is not "absolute only".
⚠ Answer-key note: pocket vetoThe key's answer is an ordinary bill that the President returned once and the Houses re-passed unchanged PYQ. Read it with care: Art 111 says the President must then give assent, but it fixes no time limit, and that gap is what the key relies on. In textbooks, a pocket veto means keeping any ordinary bill pending, as Zail Singh did in 1986. A Money Bill or Constitution Amendment Bill can never be pocket-vetoed.

  • Electoral rolls: the Election Commission (Art 324) supervises them; Electoral Registration Officers make the actual changes, and citizens can appeal exclusions. PYQ Law: RP Act 1950.
  • CGL+ First CEC: Sukumar Sen. ECI multi-member since 1993. National Voters' Day: 25 January (ECI founded 25 Jan 1950).

The Stone Age ladder

  • Flake tools dominate the Middle Palaeolithic. PYQ
  • Main Upper Palaeolithic tools: burins and scrapers (with blades). PYQ Handaxes and cleavers = Lower Palaeolithic.
  • Earliest known human form in India: Homo erectus — the Hathnora skull (Narmada valley, MP), found by Arun Sonakia, 1982. PYQ
  • Neolithic site in Jammu & Kashmir: Gufkral ("cave of the potter"). PYQ Burzahom (Kashmir) has pit houses and dogs buried with humans. CGL+ Maski (Karnataka) — Ashoka's edict there names him "Devanampiya Asoka". Bhimbetka = rock shelters (MP), UNESCO 2003, found by V.S. Wakankar. CGL+
  • Chalcolithic pair that is correct: Navdatoli — painted pottery, on the Narmada. PYQ Chalcolithic = copper + stone; no iron, no town planning. Other cultures: Ahar-Banas (Rajasthan), Kayatha, Malwa (MP), Jorwe (Maharashtra: Inamgaon, Daimabad). CGL+

Harappa: India's first cities

  • "First urbanisation" = the Indus Valley (Harappan) civilisation. PYQ The second urbanisation came in the Ganga valley, 6th century BCE (Mahajanapadas). CGL+
  • Unlike Egypt and Mesopotamia, Harappan cities lack monumental religious buildings such as temples or pyramids. PYQ They did have baked bricks, walls, grid streets and covered drains. The Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro) is the nearest thing to a ritual building.
  • CGL+ Harappa found 1921 (Daya Ram Sahni), Mohenjo-daro 1922 (R.D. Banerji). Lothal = dockyard (Gujarat). Dholavira = UNESCO 2021. Rakhigarhi (Haryana) = largest site in India.

The Vedic age

  • Vedic period is linked with the Aryans and the composition of the Vedas. PYQ
  • Rigveda was composed in Punjab and north-west India — the Sapta Sindhu (land of seven rivers). PYQ Later Vedic people moved east to the Ganga–Yamuna doab.
  • Statement check: Rigveda has hymns to Indra, Agni, Soma ✔; society was urban and used iron ✘ (it was rural, pastoral; iron came in the Later Vedic age). Answer: only statement 1. PYQ
  • CGL+ Rigveda: 10 mandalas, 1,028 hymns; most hymns to Indra (about 250), then Agni (about 200). Gayatri Mantra in Mandala 3; Purusha Sukta (four varnas) in Mandala 10. Assemblies: Sabha and Samiti.

The Guptas

Statement check PYQ Brahmanical revival and rich Sanskrit literature ✔. "Only copper coins with Prakrit legends" ✘ — the Guptas issued the largest number of gold coins (dinaras) in ancient India, with Sanskrit legends. Answer: only statement 1.
